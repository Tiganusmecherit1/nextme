# Your body: 30 trillion cells

An adult weighing 70 kg has around **30 trillion cells**. And here is the surprise: about as many bacteria live in the gut. You are, practically, half you and half "tenants".

## What a cell is, in plain words

Imagine a cell as a small factory:

- The **membrane** is the fence and the gate. It decides what comes in (glucose, amino acids, water, minerals) and what goes out (waste, carbon dioxide).
- The **nucleus** is the office with the blueprints: DNA. That is where the "recipes" for every protein you need are kept.
- **Ribosomes** are the workers who read the recipes and assemble proteins from amino acids.
- **Mitochondria** are the power plants. They burn fuel and produce energy (they get their own lesson).
- The **cytoplasm** is the water-filled "hall" where all of this floats.

## Not all cells are the same

The body has more than 200 cell types:

- **Muscle cells** are long like threads and packed with mitochondria and contractile proteins.
- **Fat cells** are like balloons filled with fat.
- **Bone cells** sit trapped in mineral and "feel" the forces pressing on the bone.
- **Red blood cells** have no nucleus; they are just bags of haemoglobin carrying oxygen.

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** red blood cells are by far the most numerous cells in the body? They make up about 84% of the total number of cells, even though they are very small.

## Why it matters to you

When we say "nutrition", we are really talking about **what your cells receive**. The protein in an egg becomes amino acids, which reach the muscle cell and are turned into new muscle. The glucose in bread enters the cells and is burned for energy. Vitamins and minerals are the "tools" without which the factory stops.

And when you train, you send the cells **signals**: "we need more strength", "we need more endurance". The cells respond by building exactly what you ask for.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** think of each meal as a delivery of materials to 30 trillion factories. Good materials (protein, vegetables, whole grains, good fats) make factories that run well.

## In short

- The body has ~30 trillion cells and about as many bacteria.
- Each cell has a gate (membrane), blueprints (DNA), workers (ribosomes) and power plants (mitochondria).
- Food and training are, at heart, materials and signals for your cells.
