# What "energy" means in the body: ATP

When you say "I have no energy", your body might agree in a very literal sense. Energy in the body has a name: **ATP** (adenosine triphosphate).

## The body's energy currency

Food contains chemical energy, measured in calories. But cells cannot "spend" a slice of bread directly. They first convert it into ATP, the same way you exchange euros for local currency before you buy something at the market.

ATP is a small molecule with three phosphate "beads" linked together. When the cell breaks the last link, energy is released. This lets:

- muscles contract,
- the heart beat,
- the brain send signals,
- cells build new proteins.

## How much ATP we use

At any moment the body holds only about **250 grams** of ATP. Yet on an ordinary day it recycles an amount of ATP **roughly equal to your body weight**. That means each molecule is reused hundreds of times a day: it is discharged, then "recharged" with energy from food.

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** a muscle has enough ATP for only a few seconds of maximal effort? That is why the body has several recharging systems that kick in one after another.

## The three recharging systems

1. **The fast system (phosphocreatine)** – lasts 8–10 seconds. It is what you use for a jump, a short sprint or a heavy rep. This is where creatine helps.
2. **The system without oxygen (glycolysis)** – burns glucose very quickly but not completely. It lasts from tens of seconds to a few minutes. It is the "burn" at the end of a set.
3. **The system with oxygen (in the mitochondria)** – burns glucose and fat completely. It is slow but almost unlimited. It keeps you alive 24 hours a day and carries you through a marathon.

In reality, all three run at the same time. Only the proportion changes with how intense the effort is.

## Why it matters

- For **strength** (short, heavy sets), the fast system matters a lot, so 2–3 minute rests between sets let it recharge.
- For long **cardio**, the mitochondria matter, along with the fuel you have: glycogen and fat.
- "Fatigue" is not just a lack of ATP. It also involves the brain, the build-up of certain substances in the muscle, and sleep. We discuss it in the lesson on body signals.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** if you feel "empty" after 30 seconds of a heavy set, it is not a lack of willpower. The fast system has simply run dry. Take longer rests between sets.

## In short

- ATP is the energy currency of cells.
- You hold little ATP in stock, but you recycle it constantly, about your body weight per day.
- Three systems recharge it: fast (seconds), without oxygen (minutes), with oxygen (hours).
