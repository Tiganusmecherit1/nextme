# Mitochondria: the power plant in every cell

If the cell is a factory, the **mitochondrion** is the power plant. This is where most of your ATP is made, the energy for everything you do.

## How it works, simply

Three things go into the mitochondrion:

- **fuel** (pieces of glucose, fat and, more rarely, protein),
- **oxygen** (from the air you breathe),
- **ADP** ("discharged" ATP).

And out come:

- charged **ATP**, ready to use,
- **carbon dioxide**, which you breathe out,
- **water**.

In effect, you breathe in to give your mitochondria oxygen, and you breathe out what is left after they have burned your food. That is why you pant during effort: the mitochondria are asking for more oxygen.

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** mitochondria have their own DNA, separate from the DNA in the nucleus? Scientists believe that more than a billion and a half years ago they were free-living bacteria that ended up living inside other cells. And you inherit them only from your mother.

## Where most of them are

Cells that work hard have thousands of mitochondria: the **heart**, **muscles**, **liver**, **brain**. Red blood cells have none at all, so they do not consume the oxygen they carry.

## You can multiply them

Here is the good part: mitochondria adapt. When you train consistently, especially cardio and interval training, your muscles get the signal "we need more energy" and **build new mitochondria** (a process called mitochondrial biogenesis). Studies show increases in muscle mitochondrial content after just a few weeks of training.

The result:

- you tire more slowly at the same effort,
- your muscles use more fat as fuel,
- blood sugar is easier to control, because the muscle "pulls" glucose out of the blood more effectively.

And the reverse: after a few weeks on the sofa, their number drops. Mitochondria follow the rule "use it or lose it".

## Mitochondria and age

Mitochondrial function declines with age, but a large part of that decline comes from **lack of movement**, not just from the years. Older adults who train have mitochondria that look much "younger" than those of sedentary people the same age.

> [!WARNING]
> **Myth vs reality:** online you will find "mitochondria supplements" promising endless energy. The best-proven way to have more and healthier mitochondria is still regular movement. For supplements, see the **Supplements** module in the app, where each one has its level of evidence.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** 150–300 minutes a week of moderate movement (brisk walking, cycling) plus 1–2 more intense sessions are enough to "grow" your mitochondria.

## In short

- The mitochondrion burns fuel with oxygen and produces ATP, CO₂ and water.
- Training, especially endurance work and intervals, increases the number of mitochondria.
- Inactivity reduces them, at any age.
