# Metabolism: how much we burn in a day

"Metabolism" is a word used for everything: "I have a slow metabolism", "I want to speed up my metabolism". In fact, metabolism means **all the chemical reactions in the body**, and the part we care about here is simple: **how much energy you spend per day**.

## The four parts of daily expenditure

1. **Basal metabolic rate (BMR)** – the energy you need just to stay alive: heart, lungs, brain, liver, kidneys, body temperature. It is usually **60–70%** of the total. Yes, you burn even while you sleep.
2. **Thermic effect of food (TEF)** – the energy spent digesting food. About **10%** of the total.
3. **NEAT** (non-exercise activity) – walking around the house, climbing stairs, gesturing, standing, cleaning. It can differ between people by **a few hundred kilocalories a day**.
4. **Exercise itself** – usually the smallest part for most people.

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** NEAT differs enormously between people? Someone who works on a building site or as a waiter can burn far more each day than someone who goes to the gym for an hour but sits for the rest of the day.

## How NextMe calculates it

For basal metabolic rate we use the **Mifflin-St Jeor** equation (1990), one of the most accurate formulas for healthy adults:

- **Men:** 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age (years) + 5
- **Women:** 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age (years) − 161

Example: a 35-year-old man, 80 kg, 180 cm has a BMR of about 800 + 1125 − 175 + 5 = **1755 kcal/day**.

We then multiply by an **activity factor** (from 1.2 for sedentary to ~1.9 for very active) to get the estimated total daily energy expenditure (TDEE).

> [!WARNING]
> **The formula is an estimate**, not a measurement. For most people it is off by no more than ~10%, but for some it can be off by more. That is why it is worth watching how your real weight changes over 2–3 weeks and adjusting calories accordingly.

## "I have a slow metabolism"

A large 2021 study (Pontzer et al., more than 6,000 people) found something unexpected: once body size is accounted for, metabolism stays **almost constant between ages 20 and 60**. It drops more clearly only after 60.

So, usually, what changes at 30–40 is not "metabolism" but that:

- we move less (lower NEAT),
- we lose muscle if we do not do strength training,
- we eat a little more, bit by bit.

The good news: all three can be controlled.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** the simplest way to burn more is not an extra hour of cardio, but **more NEAT**: 8,000–10,000 steps, stairs instead of the lift, a walk after meals. Plus strength training to keep your muscle.

## In short

- Daily expenditure = basal rate (the biggest part) + digestion + daily movement + exercise.
- NextMe uses the Mifflin-St Jeor formula; adjust the target based on how your weight actually changes.
- Between 20 and 60, metabolism does not "slow down" on its own; movement and muscle mass drop.
