# What a calorie is and why they don't all feel the same

A **kilocalorie** (kcal, what you see on the label) is the energy needed to warm one litre of water by one degree. That is all. It is a unit of measurement, like the metre or the kilogram.

## How many calories each nutrient has

The values officially used on labels in the European Union are:

| Nutrient | kcal per gram |
|---|---|
| Protein | 4 |
| Carbohydrates | 4 |
| Fibre | 2 |
| Alcohol | 7 |
| Fat | 9 |

That is why one tablespoon of oil (~10 g) has about 90 kcal, the same as a large apple.

## Energy balance

If you consistently eat **more** than you spend, the surplus is stored (mostly as fat). If you consistently eat **less**, the body takes the difference from its reserves. This is the basic rule, and no diet gets around it.

But that does not mean all calories are the same in real life.

## Three reasons the source matters

**1. Digestion costs differ.** The body spends energy processing food:

- protein: **20–30%** of its calories are "lost" to digestion,
- carbohydrates: **5–10%**,
- fat: **0–3%**.

**2. Fullness differs.** Protein, fibre and the water in foods keep you full much longer. 300 kcal of chicken breast with salad fills the stomach; 300 kcal of crisps is a handful.

**3. Ultra-processed foods make you eat more.** In a tightly controlled experiment (Hall, 2019), people ate as much as they wanted for two weeks of ultra-processed food and two weeks of minimally processed food. On the ultra-processed diet they ate **on average ~500 kcal more per day** and gained weight, even though both diets offered the same sugars, fats and fibre.

> [!WARNING]
> **Myth vs reality:** "calories don't matter, only what you eat does". False, both matter. Food quality helps you naturally eat what you need, but you can overeat healthy food too (nuts, olive oil and cheese are very calorie-dense).

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** build every meal starting from **a protein source** and **vegetables**, then add carbohydrates and fats. That way you get full on fewer calories without counting everything.

## In short

- Protein and carbohydrates: 4 kcal/g; alcohol: 7; fat: 9.
- Energy balance decides whether you lose or gain weight.
- The source of calories changes how full you feel and how much you end up eating.
