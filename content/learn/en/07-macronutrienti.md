# Macronutrients: the three fuels

"Macro" means large. **Macronutrients** are the substances in food you need in amounts of tens or hundreds of grams a day. There are three main ones:

- **Protein** – the building material.
- **Carbohydrates** – the fast fuel.
- **Fat** – the reserve fuel and the raw material for hormones and membranes.

Besides these, **water** and **fibre** are also needed in large amounts, and **alcohol** provides calories but is not a nutrient you need.

## What each one does

| Macronutrient | Main role | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Protein | Builds and repairs muscle, skin, enzymes, antibodies | eggs, chicken, fish, cheese, yoghurt, beans, lentils |
| Carbohydrates | Fast energy, especially for the brain and intense effort | bread, rice, potatoes, pasta, fruit, oats |
| Fat | Reserve energy, hormones, absorption of vitamins A, D, E, K | olive oil, nuts, seeds, oily fish, butter |

## How much of each

For healthy adults, EFSA recommends ranges, not fixed numbers:

- **Carbohydrates:** 45–60% of calories.
- **Fat:** 20–35% of calories.
- **Protein:** at least 0.83 g per kilogram of body weight per day (more if you exercise or are over 65; see the lesson on protein needs).

WHO adds: saturated fat below 10%, trans fat below 1% and free sugars below 10% of calories.

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** there is no "essential" carbohydrate? The body can make its own glucose from other sources. There are, however, essential amino acids (from protein) and essential fatty acids (from fat), which you must get from food. That does not mean a carb-free diet is better: carbohydrate foods bring fibre, vitamins and energy for training.

## There is no "bad" macronutrient

Fashions change: in the '90s fat was the enemy, then carbohydrates took the blame. Long-term studies show that **quality** matters more than the exact proportions: whole grains instead of white flour, olive oil instead of trans fats, fish and pulses instead of processed meats.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** the plate rule: **half vegetables**, **a quarter protein**, **a quarter carbohydrates** (preferably whole grain), plus a little good fat (oil, nuts, avocado).

## In short

- Three main macronutrients: protein (building), carbohydrates (fast energy), fat (reserve and hormones).
- EFSA: 45–60% carbohydrates, 20–35% fat, at least 0.83 g protein/kg.
- Food quality matters more than exact percentages.
