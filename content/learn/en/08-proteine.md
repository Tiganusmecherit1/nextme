# Protein: the body's building blocks

Think of proteins as **bead necklaces**. Each bead is an **amino acid**. There are 20 kinds of amino acids, and their combinations make tens of thousands of different proteins.

## What protein does in the body

Not just muscle. Proteins are:

- **muscles** (actin and myosin contract),
- **skin, hair, nails, tendons** (collagen and keratin),
- the **enzymes** that digest food and carry out chemical reactions,
- the **antibodies** of the immune system,
- **hormones** such as insulin and GLP-1,
- **haemoglobin**, which carries oxygen.

## What happens when you eat protein

1. In the stomach and intestine, enzymes cut the "necklace" into beads: amino acids.
2. The amino acids enter the blood.
3. Cells take them up and build exactly the proteins they need.

Your body **has no protein store** the way it has one for fat. Your proteins are constantly broken down and rebuilt. If you do not get enough from food, the body takes amino acids from muscle.

## Essential amino acids

9 of the 20 amino acids are **essential**: the body cannot make them, so they must come from food. One of them, **leucine**, is an important signal for building muscle.

- **Animal proteins** (eggs, dairy, meat, fish) contain all the essential amino acids in good proportions.
- **Plant proteins** (beans, lentils, chickpeas, soy, grains) are often lower in one or two amino acids. The solution is simple: **combine** different sources over the day (for example, beans with bread, or rice with lentils).

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** traditional Romanian fasting dishes, like bean spread with bread, bean stew or meatless stuffed cabbage, combine pulses with grains? Without knowing it, they do exactly what nutritionists recommend for plant protein.

## Cheap protein sources in Romania

| Food | Protein (approx.) |
|---|---|
| 2 eggs | 12–13 g |
| 100 g cooked chicken breast | ~30 g |
| 100 g cottage cheese | ~11–17 g (depends on product) |
| 200 g Greek yoghurt | ~16–20 g |
| 100 g cooked lentils | ~9 g |
| 100 g cooked beans | ~8 g |
| 100 g canned tuna in water | ~25 g |
| 100 g mackerel | ~19–20 g |

> [!WARNING]
> **Myth vs reality:** "lots of protein damages the kidneys". In **healthy** people, analyses of the studies found no harm to kidney function at higher protein intakes. If you already have kidney disease, the situation is different and you should talk to your nephrologist.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** put a protein source at **every meal** (20–40 g), not all your protein at dinner. Muscles use protein better when it is spread over the day.

## In short

- Proteins are chains of amino acids; 9 of them are essential.
- The body does not store protein; it recycles it constantly.
- Combine different plant sources; spread protein across every meal.
