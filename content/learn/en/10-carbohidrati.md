# Carbohydrates: sugar, starch and fibre

Carbohydrates are the most "argued about" macronutrient. Some people avoid them completely, others live on pretzels. Let us see what they really are.

## They are all made of sugars

A carbohydrate is a chain of sugar molecules. The difference is the length of the chain:

- **Simple sugars** (1–2 molecules): glucose, fructose in fruit, table sugar (sucrose), lactose in milk. They reach the blood quickly.
- **Starch** (long chains of glucose): potatoes, rice, bread, pasta, cornmeal. The body has to cut the chain, so it takes a little longer.
- **Fibre** (chains our enzymes cannot cut): it reaches the large intestine, where bacteria eat it. It has its own lesson.

## What the body does with them

1. Digestion turns starch and sugars into **glucose** (and a little fructose and galactose).
2. Glucose enters the blood, and **insulin** helps it get into cells.
3. Cells burn it for energy or store it as **glycogen**: in muscles (~400 g, varies a lot) and in the liver (~100 g).
4. Only when the stores are full and you consistently eat in surplus is some of it turned into fat.

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** your brain uses about 120 g of glucose a day? It is one of the biggest energy consumers in the body, even though it weighs only ~1.4 kg.

## Good-quality vs poor-quality carbohydrates

A large series of analyses published in *The Lancet* (2019) showed that people who eat more **fibre** and **whole grains** have a lower risk of heart disease, type 2 diabetes, stroke and colorectal cancer.

| More often | Less often |
|---|---|
| oats, buckwheat, barley, wholemeal bread | white bread, pretzels, pastries |
| beans, lentils, chickpeas, peas | sweets, cakes, biscuits |
| boiled or baked potatoes with skin | chips / fries, crisps |
| whole fruit | fruit juice, sugary drinks |
| polenta, rice (moderate portions) | sweetened breakfast cereals |

## For exercise

Carbohydrates are the preferred fuel for intense effort. If you do hard or long sessions, a very low-carb diet can reduce performance. For weight loss, what matters is total calories, not cutting out carbohydrates.

> [!WARNING]
> **Myth vs reality:** "carbs make you fat". A calorie surplus makes you fat, wherever it comes from. Studies comparing low-carb and low-fat diets, at the same calories and protein, show small differences in long-term fat loss.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** choose carbohydrates that "come packaged" with fibre: an apple instead of juice, wholemeal bread instead of white, potatoes with the skin on. And eat them after you have started the meal with vegetables and protein (see the lesson on blood sugar spikes).

## In short

- Carbohydrates are simple sugars, starch and fibre.
- Glucose is burned or stored as glycogen in muscles and liver.
- Quality matters: whole grains, pulses, whole fruit.
