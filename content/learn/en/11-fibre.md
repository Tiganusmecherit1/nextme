# Fibre: the carbohydrate you don't digest

Fibre is the part of plants that your enzymes **cannot break down**. It passes through the stomach and small intestine almost unchanged. It looks as if it does nothing. In fact, it does a lot.

## Two kinds of fibre

- **Soluble fibre** – dissolves in water and forms a kind of gel. You find it in oats, barley, beans, lentils, apples, citrus fruit and flaxseed. It slows sugar absorption and can lower cholesterol.
- **Insoluble fibre** – does not dissolve, adds "bulk" to stool and speeds transit. You find it in bran, whole grains, vegetable skins and nuts.

Most plant foods contain both types.

## What fibre does for you

1. **Fills you up.** It fills the stomach and slows its emptying.
2. **Smooths blood sugar.** The gel from soluble fibre slows the passage of sugar into the blood.
3. **Feeds your good bacteria.** In the large intestine, bacteria ferment fibre and produce **short-chain fatty acids** (such as butyrate), which feed the cells of the gut and stimulate fullness hormones such as GLP-1.
4. **Prevents constipation.**
5. **Protects you long term.** The *Lancet* analysis (2019) found the biggest benefits at **25–29 g of fibre a day** or more: lower risk of cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes and colorectal cancer.

## How much you need

EFSA recommends **at least 25 g of fibre a day** for adults. Most Europeans eat less than that.

| Food | Fibre (approx.) |
|---|---|
| 1 cup cooked beans or lentils (~180 g) | 12–15 g |
| 50 g rolled oats | 5 g |
| 2 slices wholemeal bread | 4–6 g |
| 1 medium apple with skin | 4 g |
| 1 tablespoon flaxseed | 3 g |
| 100 g cabbage | 2–3 g |
| 1 large carrot | 2 g |

> [!WARNING]
> **Caution:** increase fibre **gradually**, over 2–3 weeks, and drink enough water. If you jump suddenly from 10 g to 35 g you will get bloating and gas. Your bacteria need time to adapt.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** one serving of pulses a day (beans, lentils, chickpeas, peas) plus oats at breakfast gets you close to 25 g without effort.

## In short

- Fibre is not digested, but it feeds your gut bacteria.
- It fills you up, smooths blood sugar and lowers the risk of many diseases.
- Target: at least 25 g/day, increased gradually, with enough water.
