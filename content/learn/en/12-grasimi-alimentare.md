# Fat on your plate: why you need it

For years we were told fat is the enemy. In fact, **dietary fat** is an essential nutrient, and the problem appears only with very large amounts or the wrong types.

## What dietary fat does

- **Builds the membranes** of every one of your 30 trillion cells.
- **Is the raw material for hormones** (oestrogen, testosterone and cortisol are made from cholesterol).
- **Helps absorb vitamins A, D, E and K.** If you eat a carrot salad without a drop of oil, you absorb far less beta-carotene.
- **Provides essential fatty acids** (omega-3 and omega-6), which the body cannot make.
- **Adds flavour and fullness**, because it slows stomach emptying.

## How it is digested

Fat does not dissolve in water, so the liver sends **bile** (from the gallbladder) into the intestine. Bile "emulsifies" it, like washing-up liquid on a greasy plate, and enzymes can then cut it into fatty acids. The fatty acids are reassembled and sent through the lymph and blood to muscles, liver and fat tissue.

## How much you need

EFSA recommends that fat make up **20–35%** of calories. For someone eating 2,000 kcal, that is roughly **45–75 g of fat per day**.

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** fat has 9 kcal per gram, more than double protein and carbohydrates? That is why a "healthy" salad with 4 tablespoons of oil can have more calories than a hamburger. It is not a problem, it is just good to know.

> [!WARNING]
> **Myth vs reality:** "fat from food goes straight to your belly". Body fat builds up from the **total calorie surplus**, whether it comes from fat, carbohydrates or alcohol. Dietary fat is not "stickier" than the rest, just more calorie-dense.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** measure oil with a spoon, not "by eye". 1 tablespoon = ~10 g = ~90 kcal. Get the rest of your fat from whole foods: nuts, seeds, fish, eggs.

## In short

- Dietary fat is essential for cells, hormones and vitamins A, D, E, K.
- Target: 20–35% of calories.
- It is very calorie-dense, so measure it, but do not cut it out.
