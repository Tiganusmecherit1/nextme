# Saturated, unsaturated, trans and omega-3

All fats have 9 kcal/g, but their effects on the heart and blood vessels differ a lot. The difference comes from the shape of the molecule.

## The four families

**1. Saturated fats** – usually solid at room temperature.

- Where you find them: butter, lard, bacon, processed meats, sour cream, full-fat cheeses, coconut oil, palm oil.
- Effect: in excess they raise "bad" cholesterol (LDL).
- WHO: **below 10% of calories**.

**2. Monounsaturated fats** – liquid at room temperature.

- Where you find them: olive oil, rapeseed oil, avocado, almonds, hazelnuts.
- Effect: when they replace saturated fats, they lower LDL. The PREDIMED study (Mediterranean diet with extra-virgin olive oil or nuts) showed fewer major cardiovascular events.

**3. Polyunsaturated fats** – liquid, including **omega-6** and **omega-3**.

- Omega-6: sunflower oil, corn oil, seeds, walnuts.
- Omega-3: **oily fish** (mackerel, sprats, sardines, salmon, herring), **walnuts**, **flax and chia seeds**.
- The omega-3s from fish (EPA and DHA) matter for the heart and brain. EFSA recommends **250 mg of EPA + DHA per day**, which is about **1–2 servings of oily fish per week**.

**4. Trans fats (industrial)** – the most harmful.

- They form when oils are "partially hydrogenated" to make them solid: old-style margarines, some pastries, frying in reused oil.
- Effect: they raise LDL **and** lower HDL ("good" cholesterol). WHO links them to hundreds of thousands of cardiovascular deaths a year worldwide.
- WHO: **below 1% of calories**, ideally as close to zero as possible. In the EU, since 2021, industrial trans fats in products are limited by law to 2 g per 100 g of fat.

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** smoked mackerel or canned sardines are among the cheapest sources of omega-3 in Romanian shops? Cheaper than salmon, with as much omega-3 per serving, sometimes even more.

## What this means when shopping

| Use often | Use in moderation | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| olive oil, rapeseed oil | butter, sour cream, full-fat cheeses | products with "partially hydrogenated oils" |
| nuts, seeds | fatty pork | frying in reused oil |
| oily fish 1–2 times/week | bacon, lard | processed meats every day |

> [!WARNING]
> **Myth vs reality:** "coconut oil is a superfood". It is almost 90% saturated fat and raises LDL more than unsaturated vegetable oils. It can be used occasionally, but it is not a good choice for the heart.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** the change with the biggest effect is simple: replace some of the lard, butter and processed meats with olive or rapeseed oil, nuts and fish. You do not have to give up flavour, only frequency.

## In short

- Saturated (butter, lard, processed meats): below 10% of calories.
- Monounsaturated and omega-3 (olive oil, nuts, oily fish): good choices.
- Industrial trans fats: avoid completely.
