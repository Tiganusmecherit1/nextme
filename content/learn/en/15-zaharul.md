# Free sugar: how much is too much

Not all sugar is the same. WHO makes an important distinction:

- **Intrinsic sugars** – those in **whole** fruit and vegetables and in milk. They come "packaged" with fibre, water and nutrients. They are not a problem.
- **Free sugars** – sugar added by you or the manufacturer (in sweets, soft drinks, sauces, fruit yoghurts) **plus** the sugar in honey, syrups and **fruit juices**, even 100% natural ones.

## What WHO recommends

- **Below 10% of calories** from free sugars.
- **Ideally below 5%**, about **25 g per day** (6 teaspoons) for an adult eating 2,000 kcal.

In 2022, EFSA concluded that intake of added and free sugars should be **as low as possible** within a balanced diet.

## Where sugar hides

| Product | Sugar (approx.) |
|---|---|
| 1 can of soft drink (330 ml) | 35 g |
| 1 glass of orange juice (250 ml) | 20–25 g |
| 1 fruit yoghurt (150 g) | 15–20 g |
| 1 tablespoon ketchup | 4 g |
| 1 chocolate bar (50 g) | 25–28 g |
| 1 cream-filled pastry / croissant | 15–20 g |

A single soft drink at lunch already goes over the ideal limit for the whole day.

## Why sugary drinks are the biggest problem

Liquid calories **barely fill you up**. If you drink 300 kcal of soda, you do not eat 300 kcal less at the next meal. Studies link regular consumption of sugary drinks to weight gain, type 2 diabetes and heart disease.

> [!WARNING]
> **Myth vs reality:** "honey and brown sugar are healthier". For the body they are still free sugars, with almost the same calories. The differences in vitamins and minerals are negligible at normal amounts.

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** whole fruit is associated in studies with a **lower** risk of diabetes, while fruit juice is associated with a higher risk? Fibre and chewing make all the difference.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** the change with the biggest effect: **replace sugary drinks with water**, mineral water or unsweetened tea. For something sweet, eat the fruit, not the juice.

## In short

- The sugar in whole fruit and milk is fine.
- Free sugar (added, honey, juices): below 10% of calories, ideally under 25 g/day.
- Sugary drinks are the most problematic source.
