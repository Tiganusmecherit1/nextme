# Vitamins and minerals: what micronutrients are

"Micro" means small. **Micronutrients** are the vitamins and minerals you need in very small amounts: milligrams (mg, a thousandth of a gram) or micrograms (µg, a millionth of a gram). They do not give you energy, but without them energy cannot be used.

## What they do, by comparison

If macronutrients are **the petrol and the bricks**, micronutrients are **the screws, spark plugs and tools**. A car with a full tank will not start without spark plugs. In the same way, mitochondria cannot make ATP without B vitamins, iron and magnesium.

## Vitamins

There are 13 vitamins, divided into two groups:

- **Fat-soluble: A, D, E, K.** They are stored in the liver and in fat tissue. You need a little fat in the meal to absorb them. Because they accumulate, **high doses from supplements can be toxic** (especially A and D).
- **Water-soluble: C and the 8 B vitamins** (B1, B2, B3, B5, B6, B7, B9 – folate, B12). The body does not store much of them (except B12), so they must come from food regularly. Excess is usually eliminated in urine.

## Minerals

- **Macrominerals** (you need hundreds of mg per day): calcium, phosphorus, magnesium, sodium, potassium, chloride.
- **Trace elements**: iron, zinc, iodine, selenium, copper, manganese, fluoride, chromium, molybdenum.

Minerals build bones, transmit nerve impulses, help muscles contract and are part of enzymes and hormones.

## What people in Europe often lack

European studies show the most common shortfalls are:

- **vitamin D** (especially in winter),
- **iron** (especially in menstruating women),
- **iodine** (where salt is not iodised),
- **folate** (in women who want to become pregnant),
- **vitamin B12** (in vegans and older adults),
- **calcium and magnesium** (in people who eat little dairy, vegetables and whole grains).

Each has its own lesson in this chapter.

> [!WARNING]
> **Myth vs reality:** "more is better". Not with micronutrients. Above a certain threshold some become harmful (vitamin A, D, iron, selenium). For many of them EFSA sets a **tolerable upper intake level (UL)**. For supplement doses and cautions, see the **Supplements** module.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** the best "multivitamin" is a colourful plate. Different colours of vegetables and fruit mean different micronutrients.

## In short

- Micronutrients = vitamins and minerals, needed in small amounts.
- They do not provide energy, but without them energy cannot be used.
- Most common shortfalls: vitamin D, iron, iodine, folate, B12.
