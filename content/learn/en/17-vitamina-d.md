# Vitamin D: the sunshine vitamin

Vitamin D is special: your body can **make it itself**, in the skin, when exposed to the sun's ultraviolet B (UVB) rays. In fact, it behaves more like a hormone than a vitamin.

## What it does

- Helps the gut **absorb calcium** and phosphorus. Without it, bones cannot mineralise properly.
- Contributes to normal **muscle** function.
- Contributes to the functioning of the **immune system**.

## The winter problem

Romania lies between 44° and 48° north. At these latitudes, roughly **from October to March**, UVB rays are too weak for the skin to produce useful amounts of vitamin D, even at midday. In summer, a few tens of minutes of sun on the arms and legs a few times a week helps a lot (but without burning).

A European study (Cashman, 2016) estimated that **about 40% of Europeans** have insufficient vitamin D levels and **13%** are severely deficient.

## Who is at higher risk

- people who spend a lot of time indoors,
- older adults (the skin produces less with age),
- people with darker skin,
- people with obesity (vitamin D "hides" in fat tissue),
- people who always cover their skin.

## From food

Few foods contain much vitamin D:

- **oily fish** (salmon, mackerel, herring, sardines): the best source,
- **egg yolk**,
- **liver**,
- some fortified products (milk, cereals, margarine).

EFSA considers an intake of **15 µg (600 IU) per day** adequate for adults, assuming minimal production in the skin.

> [!CAUTION]
> Do not take high doses of vitamin D "by guesswork". Above **100 µg (4,000 IU) per day**, the tolerable upper level set by EFSA for adults, toxicity can occur (too much calcium in the blood). The safest approach: get a **25(OH)D** blood test and discuss the dose with your doctor. Supplement details are in the **Supplements** module.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** in summer, get some sun in moderation. In winter, eat oily fish 1–2 times a week and ask your GP whether you need a supplement.

## In short

- Vitamin D is made in the skin in sunlight and is essential for bones and muscles.
- In Romania, from October to March, the sun is not enough.
- Food sources: oily fish, eggs. Supplements only with tests and your doctor.
