# Iron: the oxygen carrier

Every red blood cell is full of **haemoglobin**, a protein with an atom of **iron** at its centre. Iron is what "grabs" oxygen in the lungs and releases it in muscles and organs. Iron is also found in **myoglobin** in muscle and in the enzymes of the mitochondria.

## What happens when it is lacking

First the stores empty (ferritin falls). Then **iron-deficiency anaemia** appears:

- fatigue, breathlessness on exertion,
- pallor, brittle nails, hair loss,
- dizziness, palpitations,
- reduced sports performance,
- sometimes cravings to eat ice or earth (pica).

WHO considers anaemia a major public health problem; iron deficiency is its main cause.

## How much you need

EFSA recommends:

- **adult men:** 11 mg/day,
- **women before menopause:** 16 mg/day (because of menstrual losses),
- **women after menopause:** 11 mg/day.

## Two kinds of iron

- **Haem iron** – from red meat, liver, chicken, fish. Well absorbed (15–35%).
- **Non-haem iron** – from pulses, spinach, whole grains, seeds, eggs. Absorbed less well (2–20%), but absorption can be improved.

## Absorption tricks

**Helps:**

- **vitamin C** in the same meal: bell pepper, tomatoes, sauerkraut, citrus fruit, parsley,
- a little meat or fish alongside pulses.

**Hinders:**

- **tea and coffee** drunk with the meal (tannins bind iron) – leave an hour between them and the meal,
- **calcium** in large doses (for example, calcium supplements taken with an iron-rich meal).

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** a red pepper salad next to a lentil stew can increase the iron you absorb from the lentils several times over? Vitamin C converts iron into a form that is easier to absorb.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Do not take iron supplements without tests.** Excess iron accumulates and is toxic to the liver, heart and pancreas. Some people have haemochromatosis (a genetic condition in which the body absorbs too much iron). Ask your doctor for a full blood count and ferritin.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** if you are a woman who does a lot of sport or has heavy periods, ask for a ferritin test once a year.

## In short

- Iron carries oxygen in the body; lack of it causes anaemia and fatigue.
- Women before menopause need more (16 mg/day).
- Pair plant iron with vitamin C; avoid tea and coffee with meals.
