# Magnesium and potassium: the body's electricity

Every heartbeat and every muscle contraction starts with a small **electrical impulse**. The impulse happens because electrically charged minerals (sodium, potassium, calcium, magnesium) move in and out of cells. That is why they are called **electrolytes**.

## Magnesium

**What it does:**

- takes part in **more than 300 reactions** in the body, including ATP production (ATP actually works bound to magnesium),
- helps muscles **relax** after contracting,
- contributes to nerve function, bones and blood sugar control.

**How much you need (EFSA):** 350 mg/day for men, 300 mg/day for women.

**Where you find it:** pumpkin seeds, almonds, cashews, walnuts, whole grains, oats, beans, spinach, cocoa and dark chocolate, magnesium-rich mineral water.

**Who is at risk of deficiency:** people who eat a lot of processed food and few whole grains and vegetables, people with type 2 diabetes, heavy drinkers, people on certain medications (diuretics, proton pump inhibitors).

> [!WARNING]
> **Myth vs reality:** "every cramp means magnesium deficiency". Exercise-related muscle cramps have many causes, especially **muscle fatigue**. Studies have not shown that magnesium supplements prevent cramps in most people.

## Potassium

**What it does:**

- keeps water balanced **inside** cells (sodium keeps it outside),
- is essential for **heart rhythm**,
- helps **lower blood pressure** by counterbalancing sodium.

**How much you need:** EFSA considers **3,500 mg/day** adequate for adults; WHO recommends at least 3,510 mg/day. Most people eat less.

**Where you find it:** potatoes (especially with skin), beans, lentils, bananas, tomatoes, spinach, dried apricots, yoghurt, fish, mushrooms.

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** a medium baked potato with its skin has more potassium than a banana? Potatoes are one of the cheapest potassium-rich foods in Romania.

> [!CAUTION]
> If you have kidney disease or take blood pressure medication (ACE inhibitors, ARBs, potassium-sparing diuretics), **do not take potassium supplements** without your doctor. Too much potassium in the blood can affect the heart.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** more vegetables, pulses and baked potatoes, fewer processed products. That way you raise potassium and lower sodium at the same time, the best thing for blood pressure.

## In short

- Magnesium: ATP, muscle relaxation, blood sugar; from seeds, nuts, whole grains.
- Potassium: water balance, heart rhythm, blood pressure; from potatoes, beans, vegetables.
- Potassium supplements only with your doctor.
