# Calcium: more than bones

Calcium is the most abundant mineral in the body: an adult has about **1–1.2 kg** of it. **99%** is in bones and teeth, where it gives them hardness. The remaining **1%** is small but vital.

## What "free" calcium does

- **Muscle contraction:** when the nerve gives the signal, calcium is released inside the muscle cell and triggers the "hooking" of the contracting proteins (actin and myosin). Without calcium you cannot move a finger.
- **Heartbeat.**
- **Blood clotting.**
- **Signal transmission** between nerves.

The body keeps blood calcium very tightly controlled. If it does not get enough from food, **it takes it from the bones**. Over time, this weakens the skeleton.

## Bone is a calcium bank

Think of your bones as a bank account:

- until about **25–30 years** you make deposits (you build **peak bone mass**),
- then you maintain it,
- after 40–50, and especially after menopause, withdrawals begin.

The more you deposited when young and the more you slow the withdrawals (through movement, protein, calcium and vitamin D), the lower your risk of **osteoporosis**.

## How much you need

EFSA recommends:

- **18–24 years:** 1,000 mg/day,
- **25 years and over:** 950 mg/day.

| Food | Calcium (approx.) |
|---|---|
| 1 glass of milk (250 ml) | 300 mg |
| 150 g yoghurt | 180–200 mg |
| 30 g hard cheese (cașcaval) | 200–250 mg |
| 100 g telemea (brined white cheese) | 300–450 mg (varies) |
| 100 g canned sardines (with bones) | 350 mg |
| 100 g calcium-set tofu | 350 mg or more |
| 1 tablespoon sesame seeds | ~90 mg |
| 100 g kale / broccoli | 50–150 mg |

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** vitamin D is the "key" to calcium? Without vitamin D, the gut absorbs only a small part of the calcium you eat.

> [!WARNING]
> **Myth vs reality:** "high-dose calcium supplements protect your bones". For most people, calcium from food is preferable. Excess supplements can raise the risk of kidney stones. Talk to your doctor before taking them.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** 2–3 servings of dairy a day (or fortified alternatives) plus weight training cover two of your bones' most important needs. Details in the **Bones** chapter.

## In short

- 99% of calcium is in bones; 1% makes muscles and the heart work.
- If you do not eat enough, the body takes it from your bones.
- Adults: ~950–1,000 mg/day, preferably from food; vitamin D helps absorption.
