# Salt and sodium: how much is too much

Table salt is **sodium chloride**. Sodium is an essential electrolyte: it keeps water **outside** cells, helps nerves transmit signals and muscles contract. The problem is not a lack of it, but **excess**.

## What WHO recommends

- **Less than 5 g of salt per day** (about one level teaspoon), that is **less than 2 g of sodium**.
- WHO estimates that most adults worldwide eat on average **almost double** this amount.

## Why it matters

Sodium pulls water with it. Too much sodium means more blood volume and more pressure on the vessels. Over time this raises the risk of:

- **high blood pressure**,
- **stroke**,
- **heart and kidney disease**.

WHO considers salt reduction one of the most effective public health measures.

## Where salt comes from

The surprise: most salt does not come from the salt shaker but from **ready-made foods**:

| Food | Salt (approx.) |
|---|---|
| 100 g white bread | 1–1.5 g |
| 100 g salami / luncheon meat | 2–3 g |
| 100 g telemea (brined white cheese) | 2–4 g (varies a lot) |
| 100 g sauerkraut / pickles | 1.5–3 g |
| 1 packet of instant soup | 3–5 g |
| 1 small pizza | 3–5 g |
| 100 g olives | 3–4 g |

On EU labels, the amount of **salt** is printed directly, not sodium.

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** your taste for salt can "reset"? If you gradually cut salt for a few weeks, your taste buds adapt, and very salty food starts to taste too salty.

## Exercise and salt

If you sweat a lot (long sessions, summer, endurance sport), you also lose sodium. In these cases a normal, lightly salted meal after exercise is usually enough. Electrolyte drinks are useful mainly for efforts longer than 60–90 minutes in the heat.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** taste before you salt, use herbs (dill, parsley, thyme, lovage), garlic and lemon for flavour, and read the label on processed meats, cheeses and bread.

## In short

- WHO: less than 5 g of salt/day (one level teaspoon).
- Excess raises blood pressure and the risk of stroke and heart attack.
- Most of it comes from bread, processed meats, cheeses, pickles and ready meals.
