# Iodine, zinc and selenium: the little guardians

You need these minerals in **micrograms or a few milligrams** a day, but their absence is felt throughout the body.

## Iodine: fuel for the thyroid

**What it does:** the thyroid uses iodine to produce **thyroid hormones** (T3 and T4). These hormones set the pace of metabolism, body temperature, heart rate and, in children, brain development.

**What happens when it is lacking:** the thyroid enlarges to compensate (goitre), and fatigue, feeling cold and weight gain appear. In pregnancy, iodine deficiency can affect the baby's brain development.

**How much you need:** EFSA considers **150 µg/day** adequate for adults and 200 µg/day in pregnancy and breastfeeding.

**Sources:** **iodised salt**, sea fish, seafood, dairy, eggs.

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** the mountain areas of Romania (the Carpathians) were historically areas of endemic goitre because of iodine-poor soil? Iodising table salt greatly reduced this problem. Even if you cut back on salt, choose the **iodised** kind.

## Zinc: the immune system's mechanic

**What it does:** it is part of **hundreds of enzymes**, helps the immune system, wound healing, taste and smell, testosterone production and sperm formation.

**How much you need:** EFSA gives values between about **7.5 and 12.7 mg/day** for women and **9.4–16.3 mg/day** for men, depending on how much phytate (substances in whole grains and pulses that reduce absorption) the diet contains.

**Sources:** red meat, chicken, seafood (oysters are the champions), eggs, dairy, pumpkin seeds, cashews, pulses, whole grains.

**Who is at risk:** vegetarians, older adults, people with digestive diseases, heavy drinkers.

## Selenium: the cells' shield

**What it does:** it is part of enzymes that **protect cells from oxidative damage** and helps activate thyroid hormones.

**How much you need:** EFSA considers **70 µg/day** adequate for adults.

**Sources:** fish, eggs, meat, grains. **Brazil nuts** are extremely rich: 1–2 nuts can cover a day's needs.

> [!CAUTION]
> Selenium has a narrow margin between a good dose and a toxic one. Do not eat many Brazil nuts every day and do not combine several selenium-containing supplements. The tolerable upper level set by EFSA for adults is 255 µg/day. If you have a thyroid condition, do not take iodine or selenium supplements without your endocrinologist.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** iodised salt (a little), fish 1–2 times a week, eggs, a handful of pumpkin seeds or nuts, and a varied diet usually cover all three.

## In short

- Iodine: thyroid hormones; use iodised salt.
- Zinc: immunity, healing, hormones; from meat, eggs, seeds, pulses.
- Selenium: cell protection; 1–2 Brazil nuts, fish, eggs. Watch out for excess.
