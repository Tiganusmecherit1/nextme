# Water in the body: inside and outside the cell

An adult man's body is about **60% water**, a woman's about **50–55%** (women have on average more fat and less muscle, and fat holds little water). For a 70 kg person, that is **about 40 litres**.

## Where the water is

Imagine the body as a city:

- **Intracellular water** (inside cells) – about **two thirds** of the total. This is "cellular water". Each cell is like a house full of fluid, where chemical reactions take place.
- **Extracellular water** (outside cells) – about **one third**. It includes blood **plasma** (the streets everything travels on) and **interstitial fluid** between cells (the yards between the houses).

Water moves constantly between these spaces, pulled by minerals: **potassium** keeps water inside cells, **sodium** keeps it outside.

## What water does

- **Transports** nutrients, oxygen, hormones and waste.
- **Cools the body** through sweat. During intense effort you can lose 1–2 litres of sweat per hour.
- **Is the medium** in which all chemical reactions happen, including ATP production.
- **Lubricates joints** and protects the brain and spinal cord.
- **Removes waste** through the kidneys.

## Where most water is found

| Tissue | Water (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Blood (plasma) | ~90% |
| Muscle | ~75% |
| Brain | ~75% |
| Bone | ~20–30% |
| Fat tissue | ~10–20% |

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** muscle is one of the "wettest" parts of the body? When you build muscle, you also increase the amount of water in your body. That is why, early in a training programme, the scale may go up a little even though you are not gaining fat.

## Cellular water and cell hydration

When a cell is well hydrated, it works better: it builds proteins, holds glycogen and keeps its volume. Some research suggests cell volume is itself a signal: a "swollen" cell leans towards building, a "shrunken" one towards breaking down. It is still an area of study, so we will not exaggerate, but it shows why hydration matters for muscle too.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** intracellular water rises with muscle mass. Strength training, protein and potassium (vegetables, potatoes, beans) help cells hold water where it belongs.

## In short

- The body is ~50–60% water: ~40 L in a 70 kg adult.
- Two thirds is inside cells, one third in the blood and between cells.
- Potassium keeps water inside cells, sodium outside.
