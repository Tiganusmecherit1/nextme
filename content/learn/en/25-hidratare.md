# What happens when you don't drink enough

The body loses water continuously: through urine, sweat, breathing (exhaled air is moist) and stool. Normally about **2–3 litres a day**, more in summer and during exercise.

## The thirst signal

When you lose water, the blood becomes more "concentrated". The brain (hypothalamus) senses this and does two things:

1. It makes you **thirsty**.
2. It sends **antidiuretic hormone (ADH)** to the kidneys, which save water and make urine darker.

Thirst usually appears when you have already lost **1–2% of your body weight** in water. In older adults the sense of thirst is weaker, so the risk of dehydration is higher.

## Effects of dehydration

| Water loss (of body weight) | What you feel |
|---|---|
| 1–2% | thirst, weaker concentration, tiredness, sometimes headaches |
| ~2% or more | lower aerobic performance, higher heart rate at the same effort, poorer temperature control |
| 3–5% | dizziness, cramps, marked weakness |
| over 6–10% | medical emergency |

For an 80 kg person, 2% is **1.6 kg** of water, easy to lose in an hour of training in summer.

> [!WARNING]
> **Myth vs reality:** "if you're thirsty, it's already too late". For healthy adults in everyday life, thirst is a fairly good signal. The exceptions are older adults, young children, long efforts in the heat and some illnesses.

## How to check your hydration

The simplest method: **urine colour**.

- pale straw yellow = hydrated,
- dark yellow, amber = drink more,
- completely clear all the time = you are probably drinking more than needed.

(B vitamins can colour urine bright yellow, unrelated to hydration.)

## And too much water?

Yes, it exists. If you drink a lot in a short time (for example, several litres in an hour, especially during long exercise without salt), the sodium in your blood becomes diluted. The condition is called **hyponatraemia** and can be dangerous. A healthy adult's kidneys can eliminate about **0.8–1 litre per hour**.

> [!CAUTION]
> If you have heart failure, kidney disease or your doctor has restricted your fluids, follow your doctor's advice, not the general target in the app.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** weigh yourself before and after a long session. Each kilogram lost is roughly one litre of sweat. Gradually drink about 1.25–1.5 litres for every kilogram lost, over the following hours.

## In short

- You lose 2–3 L of water a day, more in heat and exercise.
- A loss of ~2% of body weight already lowers performance and concentration.
- Pale yellow urine is a good sign; do not drink litres in excess in a short time.
