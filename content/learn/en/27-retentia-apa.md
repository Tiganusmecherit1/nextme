# Water retention and the scale that jumps

You weigh yourself on Monday: 80 kg. Tuesday: 81.3 kg. "Did I gain over a kilo in a day?" No. To gain 1 kg of fat you would need a surplus of roughly 7,000–7,700 kcal. Most likely, it is **water**.

## The usual causes

**1. Carbohydrates and glycogen.** Each gram of glycogen (the sugar stored in muscles and liver) is stored together with about **3 grams of water**. An evening of lots of pasta or a holiday meal fills the stores, and the scale goes up 1–2 kg. Conversely, in the first days of a low-carb diet you quickly lose 1–3 kg, mostly water, not fat.

**2. Salt.** A very salty meal (processed meats, pickles, restaurant food) makes the body hold on to water to dilute the sodium. The effect passes in 1–3 days.

**3. Training.** After a new or very hard session, muscles become slightly inflamed and hold water for repairs. Muscle glycogen also rises with training.

**4. The menstrual cycle.** Many women retain water in the days before their period and in its first days, because of hormonal changes. The scale may show 1–2 kg more.

**5. Stress and poor sleep.** High cortisol can encourage water retention.

**6. Digestion.** Simply put, the food and drink in your gut weigh something. A large lunch can weigh 1 kg.

## How to weigh yourself properly

- **In the morning**, after the toilet, before eating or drinking.
- Under the same conditions (same clothes or none).
- Follow the **7-day average**, not a single day's number.
- Compare weeks, not days.

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** the rapid "weight" loss in the first days of a miracle diet is mostly water from glycogen? That is why it comes back just as quickly once the diet ends.

> [!WARNING]
> **Myth vs reality:** "to get rid of water retention I need to drink less water". It is usually the opposite: when you drink enough consistently, the body has no reason to save water. Persistent retention, with swelling of the legs or face, should however be checked by a doctor.

> [!CAUTION]
> Sudden swelling of the legs (especially just one, with pain), shortness of breath or a swollen face are not "normal retention". See a doctor.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** also log your **waist** (waist circumference) in NextMe once a week. If your waist goes down while your weight stalls, you are losing fat and gaining muscle or water.

## In short

- Day-to-day swings of 1–2 kg are almost always water.
- Glycogen, salt, training and the menstrual cycle move the scale.
- Watch the 7-day average and your waist, not one morning's number.
