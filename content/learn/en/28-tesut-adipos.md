# Fat tissue: an organ, not a dead storeroom

For a long time body fat was seen as a simple "warehouse" of calories. Since the '90s we have known that **fat tissue is an endocrine organ**: it produces hormones and substances that affect the whole body.

## What it is made of

Fat tissue consists mainly of **adipocytes**, cells that look like balloons filled almost completely with one large droplet of fat (triglycerides). Among them are blood vessels, nerves, immune cells and "stem" cells that can become new adipocytes.

## What it does

**1. Energy store.** A lean 70 kg person with 15% body fat has ~10 kg of fat, roughly **75,000–90,000 kcal** stored. In theory, enough for several weeks without food. It is the reserve humans relied on for thousands of years during famines.

**2. Insulation and protection.** It keeps you warm and protects organs such as the kidneys.

**3. A hormone factory.** The most important ones:

- **Leptin** – discovered in 1994. It tells the brain "we have enough reserves". The more fat you have, the more leptin you produce. (More in the lesson on hunger hormones.)
- **Adiponectin** – increases insulin sensitivity and has anti-inflammatory effects. Paradoxically, it falls when fat increases a lot.
- **Oestrogen** – fat tissue converts other hormones into oestrogen, which is why after menopause it becomes an important source of oestrogen.
- **Inflammatory substances** (cytokines) – in excess, especially from abdominal fat, they contribute to mild, chronic inflammation throughout the body.

## When fat becomes a problem

A little fat is essential: women need about **10–13%** essential fat (for hormones and reproduction), men **2–5%**. Problems appear when:

- there is **too much** in total,
- it accumulates **in the wrong places** (around organs, in the liver, in muscle),
- adipocytes become **too large** and "suffer": they become inflamed and release fat into the blood.

WHO estimates that in 2022 **more than 2.5 billion adults** worldwide were overweight, more than 890 million of them living with obesity.

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** people who cannot produce fat tissue at all (a rare condition called lipodystrophy) end up with severe diabetes and fatty liver? Without a safe place to store it, fat goes into the liver and muscles. This shows the problem is not fat itself, but **where** and **how much**.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** do not "hate" your body fat. It is an organ doing a job. The goal is not zero fat, but **a healthy amount in the right places**.

## In short

- Fat tissue is an organ that stores energy and produces hormones.
- Leptin, adiponectin and oestrogen come partly from fat.
- The problem is excess fat and fat in the wrong places, not fat itself.
