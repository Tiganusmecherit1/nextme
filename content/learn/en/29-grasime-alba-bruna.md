# White, brown and beige fat

Not all body fat is the same. There are three types, with almost opposite roles.

## White fat: the storeroom

- It is **most** of the fat in the body.
- Its adipocytes have **a single large droplet** of fat and few mitochondria.
- Role: **stores** energy and produces hormones (leptin, adiponectin).
- This is the fat on the belly, hips, thighs and around the organs.

## Brown fat: the stove

- Its adipocytes have **many small droplets** of fat and **lots of mitochondria**. The iron in the mitochondria gives it its reddish-brown colour.
- Role: **burns** fat and glucose to produce **heat**, not ATP. It has a special protein (UCP1) that "short-circuits" the mitochondrion: the energy comes out directly as heat.
- Babies have quite a lot of brown fat, so they stay warm without shivering.

For a long time it was thought adults no longer had brown fat. In 2009, several studies published in *NEJM* showed that **adults do have it**, mainly around the neck, collarbones and along the spine, and that it **switches on in the cold**.

## Beige fat: in between

- These are white adipocytes that, with prolonged cold or other signals, can take on brown-fat characteristics (a process called "browning").
- Researchers are studying how they might be used to treat obesity and diabetes.

## Can brown fat make you lose weight?

It sounds nice, but **adults have little brown fat** (usually tens to a few hundred grams), and the energy it burns is modest compared with total expenditure. Regular cold exposure can increase it and may slightly improve insulin sensitivity, but it is **not an effective weight-loss method** on its own.

> [!WARNING]
> **Myth vs reality:** "cold showers melt fat". Cold activates brown fat and can wake you up, but its effect on weight is small. A calorie deficit and movement remain far more important.

> [!CAUTION]
> Extreme cold exposure (ice baths, cold-water swimming) can be dangerous if you have heart disease or high blood pressure. Ask your doctor first.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** you do not need to live in the cold. But a cool bedroom (18–19°C) is good both for sleep and for brown fat.

## In short

- White fat stores energy, brown fat burns it to make heat.
- Adults have a little brown fat, activated by cold.
- Its effect on weight is small; it does not replace diet and movement.
