# Fat under the skin vs fat around the organs

Two people can have the same weight and the same body-fat percentage but very different health risks. The difference is **where** the fat is.

## Subcutaneous fat

- It is the fat **under the skin**, the kind you can pinch: on the belly, hips, thighs, arms.
- It makes up about **80–90%** of body fat.
- Fat on the **hips and thighs** (the "pear" shape) even seems to partly protect metabolism: it is a safe storage place.

## Visceral fat

- It is the fat **inside the abdomen**, around the liver, intestines and kidneys. You cannot pinch it; the belly is firm and round (the "apple" shape).
- Although there is less of it, it is **much more active**: it releases fatty acids and inflammatory substances **directly into the vein that leads to the liver**.
- It is strongly associated with **insulin resistance**, **type 2 diabetes**, **fatty liver**, **high blood pressure** and **heart disease**.

## Ectopic fat

This is fat that ends up **inside organs**: in the liver (steatosis, "fatty liver"), in muscles, in the pancreas, around the heart. It appears mostly when subcutaneous fat tissue is "full" and the surplus has nowhere else to go.

## What increases visceral fat

- a constant calorie surplus,
- inactivity,
- excess alcohol,
- short sleep and chronic stress,
- age and genetics,
- in women, menopause (fat shifts from the hips to the abdomen).

## How to measure it at home

The simplest way: **a tailor's tape measure**, at the level of the belly button, in the morning, after a normal breath out.

| Waist | Risk |
|---|---|
| Men over 94 cm / Women over 80 cm | increased risk |
| Men over 102 cm / Women over 88 cm | substantially increased risk |

Another good indicator: **your waist should be less than half your height** (waist-to-height ratio below 0.5). For someone 170 cm tall, the waist should be under 85 cm.

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** visceral fat is usually **among the first to go** when you exercise and lose weight? Even with modest weight loss, fat around the organs can fall proportionally more than fat under the skin.

## In short

- Subcutaneous fat is under the skin; visceral fat is around the organs and is more dangerous.
- Visceral fat is linked to diabetes, fatty liver and heart disease.
- Measure your waist: ideally under half your height.
