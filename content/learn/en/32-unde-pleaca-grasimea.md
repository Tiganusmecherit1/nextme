# Where fat goes when you lose weight

Ask ten people where fat goes when you lose weight and you will hear: "it gets burned", "it turns into energy", "it leaves through sweat", "it turns into muscle". The correct answer is surprising: **you breathe it out**.

## The calculation

In 2014, two Australian researchers (Meerman and Brown) published the exact calculation in the *BMJ*. A molecule of fat (a triglyceride) is made of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen. When it is burned completely in the mitochondria, with the oxygen you breathe, it turns into:

- **carbon dioxide (CO₂)** – which you breathe out,
- **water (H₂O)** – which you lose through urine, sweat and breath.

The result: of **10 kg of fat** lost,

- about **8.4 kg leaves through the lungs**, as CO₂,
- about **1.6 kg becomes water**.

The lungs are, in practice, **the main organ for getting rid of fat**.

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** a person at rest breathes out about 200 g of carbon a day? That means part of every breath you take is, literally, fat and food that has been burned.

## So why not just breathe faster to lose weight?

Because breathing is not the cause, it is the **effect**. You breathe out more CO₂ only when your mitochondria burn more fuel, that is, when you **spend more energy than you eat**. If you breathe hard without effort, you just get dizzy (hyperventilate).

## Fat does not turn into muscle

Fat and muscle are completely different tissues, made of different molecules. Fat does not "become" muscle, and muscle does not "become" fat when you stop training. One can simply go down while the other goes up.

## Sweat is not fat

Sweat is **water and salts** that cool the body. You can sweat enormously in a sauna while burning almost nothing. The weight you lose comes back as soon as you drink water.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** for your lungs to "remove" fat, you need a calorie deficit: a little less food and a little more movement. Every extra walk means, literally, more fat breathed out.

## In short

- Burned fat becomes CO₂ (~84%) and water (~16%).
- Most of it you breathe out through your lungs.
- Fat does not turn into muscle and does not leave through sweat.
