# Fat cells shrink, they don't disappear

When you gain or lose weight, two things could change: the **number** of fat cells or their **size**. In adults, it is almost only the size that changes.

## What a famous study showed

In 2008, Swedish researchers (Spalding et al., *Nature*) used a clever method: they measured traces of radioactive carbon left in the atmosphere by nuclear tests in the '50s and '60s, which had been "written" into the DNA of cells. That way they could calculate **how old people's fat cells are**.

What they found:

- **The number of adipocytes is set in childhood and adolescence** and stays almost constant in adults, even after major weight loss.
- The cells **renew**: about **10% a year** die and are replaced. But the total number stays the same.
- People with obesity since childhood generally have **more** adipocytes.

## What this means for you

- **When you gain weight**, adipocytes swell like balloons. Only when they become very large and cannot grow further may the body make new cells.
- **When you lose weight**, the balloon deflates. The cell stays, smaller, ready to fill up again.

This partly explains why **maintenance** after weight loss is hard: the emptied adipocytes send signals (through less leptin) that reserves are low, and the brain increases hunger and slightly lowers energy expenditure.

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** very large adipocytes are the "sickest"? They become inflamed, insulin-resistant and release fat into the blood. Metabolically, having more, smaller adipocytes is better than having a few huge ones.

## It is not a sentence

The fact that the cells remain does not mean you will regain the weight. It only means that the **habits** that helped you lose weight need to stay too. People who keep weight off long term usually have a few things in common: they move a lot, eat enough protein and fibre, weigh themselves regularly and sleep enough.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** after losing weight, do not suddenly go back to "the food you ate before". Increase calories gradually, by 100–200 kcal a week, and keep up your strength training.

## In short

- In adults, the number of fat cells is almost constant.
- When you lose weight, the cells shrink, they do not disappear.
- Maintenance requires lasting habits, not just a temporary diet.
