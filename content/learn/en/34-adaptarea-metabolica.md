# Why weight loss slows down (metabolic adaptation)

The first weeks of a diet usually go well. Then, although you are doing the same things, the scale stops. You are not imagining it: the body **adapts**.

## What changes when you lose weight

**1. A smaller body spends less.** A body that is 10 kg lighter needs less energy to live and move. That is pure physics.

**2. "Hidden" movement (NEAT) drops.** Without realising it, you move less: you sit more, gesture less, take fewer steps.

**3. Adaptation proper.** On top of the above, metabolism can drop **more than the weight lost would explain**. Muscles become more efficient, thyroid hormones and leptin fall.

**4. Hunger rises.** Leptin falls (the brain thinks reserves are running out), ghrelin (the hunger hormone) rises, and food looks more appealing.

## How big the effect is

The best-known study is of contestants on the TV show "The Biggest Loser", who lost a very large amount of weight in very little time. Six years later, their resting metabolism was on average **~500 kcal/day lower** than expected for their weight. This is an **extreme** case (very fast, very large weight loss). For most people who lose weight moderately, adaptation is much smaller, on the order of **a few tens up to ~100–200 kcal/day**.

> [!WARNING]
> **Myth vs reality:** "I've ruined my metabolism forever". Metabolism does not "break". It adapts to a smaller body and less food. The adaptation shrinks when you go back to enough food and keep your muscle mass.

## How to manage adaptation

1. **Moderate deficit.** Lose about **0.5–1% of your body weight per week**, no more. Very fast weight loss amplifies adaptation and muscle loss.
2. **Strength training.** It preserves muscle, the most "expensive" tissue in terms of energy.
3. **Enough protein** (1.6–2.2 g/kg in a deficit). It protects muscle and keeps you full.
4. **Daily steps.** Set a step target so NEAT does not drop without you noticing.
5. **Diet breaks.** Some people benefit from 1–2 weeks at maintenance calories after 8–12 weeks of deficit. It helps mostly psychologically.
6. **Recalculate.** Every 4–5 kg lost, update your weight in your profile so NextMe recalculates your calorie needs.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** if your average weight stays flat **for more than 2–3 weeks**, check the simple things first: portions "by eye", weekends, oil, drinks, steps. Only then cut calories by ~100–200 kcal.

## In short

- When you lose weight, the body spends less and you get hungrier.
- Adaptation is real but usually modest; extreme cases come from extreme weight loss.
- A moderate deficit, strength training, protein and steps keep it in check.
