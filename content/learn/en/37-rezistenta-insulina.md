# Insulin resistance: when the key stops working

Let us go back to the key-and-lock comparison. In **insulin resistance**, the lock has rusted. The key (insulin) goes in, but turns with difficulty. Glucose stays longer in the street (in the blood).

## What the body does

The pancreas notices that blood sugar is not falling enough and does what anyone would: it **makes more insulin**. For years, this works. Blood sugar looks normal on tests, but insulin is 2–3 times higher than it should be.

Over time, the pancreas's beta cells get tired. When they can no longer keep up, blood sugar starts to rise: first **prediabetes**, then **type 2 diabetes**.

## Why it happens

The main causes:

1. **Excess fat, especially visceral and ectopic fat** (in the liver and muscles). Fat inside muscle and liver cells blocks the insulin signal.
2. **Inactivity.** Muscles that do not work become "lazy" with insulin. Even a few days of bed rest reduce insulin sensitivity.
3. **Low muscle mass.** Less "sponge" for glucose.
4. **Short sleep and chronic stress.**
5. **Genetics and age.** A family history of diabetes raises the risk.
6. **Some conditions and medications:** polycystic ovary syndrome, corticosteroids.

## Possible signs

Insulin resistance **does not hurt** and often gives no clear signs. A few clues:

- **a large waist** (men over 94 cm, women over 80 cm),
- **dark, velvety patches** of skin on the neck or in the armpits (acanthosis nigricans),
- **high blood pressure**, high triglycerides, low HDL,
- **fatty liver** on ultrasound,
- sleepiness and strong hunger after carbohydrate-heavy meals,
- **polycystic ovaries**, in women.

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** that in Romania the national PREDATORR study estimated that about **1 in 9 adults** has diabetes and **almost 1 in 5** has prediabetes? Many of them do not know it.

## The very good news: it is reversible

Insulin resistance **responds very well to lifestyle**. In the large American Diabetes Prevention Program (2002), people with prediabetes who lost **~7% of their weight** and did **150 minutes of exercise a week** cut their risk of developing diabetes by **58%** over about 3 years. More than the drug tested (metformin, -31%).

What works best:

- **strength training** (more muscle, more "sponge"),
- **daily movement** and walks after meals,
- **losing abdominal fat**, even 5–10% of body weight,
- **sleeping 7+ hours**,
- **more fibre and less free sugar**.

> [!CAUTION]
> If you have a large waist, a family history of diabetes or are over 40–45, ask your GP for a fasting blood sugar test and HbA1c (see the lesson on lab tests).

## In short

- In insulin resistance, cells respond weakly and the pancreas compensates with more insulin.
- Main causes: visceral fat, inactivity, little muscle, short sleep.
- It is reversible: exercise and ~7% weight loss cut diabetes risk by more than half.
