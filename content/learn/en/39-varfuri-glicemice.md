# 8 proven ways to reduce blood sugar spikes

Blood sugar rises after a meal in everyone. But very large, repeated spikes put strain on the pancreas and are associated with getting hungry again soon after eating and, long term, with metabolic risk. Here is what you can do, from the most effective to the simplest.

## 1. Walk for 10–15 minutes after a meal

Working muscles take glucose out of the blood **even without insulin**. An analysis of studies (Buffey, 2022) showed that breaking up sitting with **light walking** lowers blood sugar after a meal more than standing does. The effect is biggest if you move in the **first 30–60 minutes** after eating.

## 2. Eat in this order: vegetables → protein → carbohydrates

In one study (Shukla, 2015), when participants ate the vegetables and protein **first** and the bread and juice **last**, their blood sugar after the meal was about **29–37% lower** than in the reverse order, with the same food. Fibre and protein slow stomach emptying and switch on the hormone GLP-1.

## 3. Put fibre in every meal

Soluble fibre forms a gel that slows the passage of sugar into the blood. A salad, a vegetable soup or a serving of beans alongside the meal makes a difference.

## 4. Don't eat "naked" carbohydrates

A pretzel on its own raises blood sugar much more sharply than a wholemeal sandwich with egg and vegetables. **Combine** carbohydrates with protein, good fats and fibre.

## 5. Do strength training regularly

More muscle means a bigger "sponge" for glucose. The American Diabetes Association recommends strength exercises **2–3 times a week**, in addition to aerobic activity, for blood sugar control.

## 6. Sleep 7 hours or more

In a classic study (Spiegel, 1999), healthy young people who slept only **4 hours a night for 6 nights** had much lower glucose tolerance, close to that of some people at risk of diabetes. After a few nights of recovery sleep, their values improved.

## 7. Vinegar (modest effect)

1–2 tablespoons of vinegar (apple cider or wine), diluted, in a salad or before a carbohydrate-rich meal, may slightly reduce the glucose spike, according to small studies (Johnston, 2004). The effect is real but **modest**. Do not drink it neat and undiluted (it damages tooth enamel and the stomach).

## 8. Choose less processed carbohydrates

Oats instead of sweetened cereals, pasta al dente, boiled and cooled potatoes (potato salad), real wholemeal bread, whole fruit instead of juice.

> [!WARNING]
> **Myth vs reality:** "every blood sugar spike is dangerous". In healthy people, a rise after meals is normal. You do not need a glucose sensor to live healthily. The goal is to avoid very large, repeated spikes, not to keep your blood sugar flat.

> [!CAUTION]
> If you have diabetes and take medication, these methods can lower your blood sugar. Tell your doctor what you are changing so they can adjust your treatment if needed.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** the easiest combination: **start the meal with salad or soup**, eat the bread last and **take a 10-minute walk** afterwards.

## In short

- A walk after meals and the order "vegetables → protein → carbohydrates" have the biggest effect.
- Fibre, strength training and 7+ hours of sleep help long term.
- Vinegar helps a little; normal spikes are not dangerous in healthy people.
