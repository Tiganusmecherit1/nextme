# Lab tests: blood sugar, HbA1c and prediabetes

Type 2 diabetes and prediabetes can exist for years without any symptoms. The only sure way to know is a **blood test**.

## The most important tests

**1. Fasting blood glucose** (after at least 8 hours without food)

| Result (mg/dL) | What it means |
|---|---|
| 70–99 | normal |
| 100–125 | impaired fasting glucose (prediabetes, by ADA criteria; WHO uses 110–125) |
| 126 or higher (confirmed) | diabetes |

**2. HbA1c (glycated haemoglobin)**

It shows **the average blood sugar over the last 2–3 months**. Glucose sticks to the haemoglobin in red blood cells, and these live about 120 days. It is like a "long-exposure photo" of blood sugar. You do not need to be fasting.

| HbA1c | What it means |
|---|---|
| below 5.7% | normal |
| 5.7–6.4% | prediabetes (ADA) |
| 6.5% or higher (confirmed) | diabetes |

**3. Oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT)**

You drink 75 g of glucose dissolved in water, and your blood sugar is measured after 2 hours. It is more sensitive than fasting glucose. Values of 140–199 mg/dL indicate impaired glucose tolerance (prediabetes); 200 or higher, diabetes.

**4. Fasting insulin and the HOMA-IR index** (optional)

Some doctors use them to estimate insulin resistance. They are not standard diagnostic tests, and reference values differ between labs.

## Who should get tested

The ADA recommends testing:

- all adults from **age 35**, repeated at least every 3 years if normal,
- earlier, at any age, if you are **overweight** and have at least one risk factor: a first-degree relative with diabetes, inactivity, high blood pressure, abnormal cholesterol or triglycerides, polycystic ovaries, past diabetes in pregnancy.

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** prediabetes is not "almost diabetes that will come anyway"? Many people with prediabetes return to normal values after losing a little weight and moving more. It is a warning signal, not a sentence.

> [!CAUTION]
> NextMe **cannot make a diagnosis**. If your values are outside the normal ranges, discuss the results with your GP or a diabetes specialist. Results can vary between labs.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** at your next annual check-up, ask for **fasting glucose, HbA1c, a lipid profile and ALT/AST** (for the liver). Keep the results so you can see how they change over time.

## In short

- Normal fasting glucose: 70–99 mg/dL; normal HbA1c: below 5.7%.
- HbA1c shows the average of the last 2–3 months.
- Prediabetes is reversible; get tested from 35, or earlier if you have risk factors.
