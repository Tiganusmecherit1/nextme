# The microbiome: the garden in your gut

About **38 trillion bacteria** live in your large intestine, roughly as many as the cells in your body. Together with viruses, fungi and other microorganisms, they make up the **gut microbiome**. It weighs about 200 grams.

## A garden, not a war

For a long time we thought bacteria were enemies. In fact, most of those in the gut are **partners**. Think of the microbiome as a **garden**:

- **The good flowers** are bacteria that ferment fibre and produce useful substances.
- **The weeds** are bacteria that, in excess, cause inflammation.
- **The fertiliser** is what you eat: fibre feeds the flowers, ultra-processed food feeds the weeds more.
- **Diversity** is the key. A garden with hundreds of species is more resilient than one with three.

## What bacteria do for you

1. **Digest fibre** your enzymes cannot cut, turning it into **short-chain fatty acids** (butyrate, propionate, acetate).
2. **Feed the gut wall.** Butyrate is the main fuel of the cells lining the colon.
3. **Train the immune system.** About 70% of immune cells are in or around the gut.
4. **Make vitamins**, for example vitamin K and some B vitamins.
5. **Keep dangerous bacteria away** by taking up space and resources.
6. **Influence blood sugar, hunger and even mood** (next lessons).

## What we know for sure, and what we don't

Microbiome science is young and very active. **We know for sure** that:

- diets rich in fibre and diverse plants are associated with a more diverse microbiome,
- antibiotics temporarily disrupt the microbiome,
- the microbiome differs enormously between people.

**We do not yet know exactly** what the "ideal microbiome" is, and no commercial test can tell you precisely what to eat. Be careful with products that promise this.

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** every person has an almost unique microbiome, like a fingerprint? Even identical twins have different microbiomes.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** the simplest way to have a rich garden is **variety**: as many different plants as possible each week (vegetables, fruit, pulses, whole grains, nuts, seeds, herbs). We talk about the "30 plants rule" in the next lesson.

## In short

- The microbiome = ~38 trillion bacteria in the gut, as many as your own cells.
- They digest fibre, feed the gut and train immunity.
- Diversity is the key, and fibre is the fertiliser.
