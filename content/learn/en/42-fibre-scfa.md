# How your bacteria feed you: fibre and 30 plants a week

Fibre reaches the large intestine almost untouched. There, bacteria **ferment** it, the way yeast ferments dough. This fermentation produces a few substances that are very important for you.

## Short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs)

The three main ones:

- **Butyrate** – the main food of colon cells. It helps keep the gut wall "tight" and has anti-inflammatory effects.
- **Propionate** – reaches the liver and helps regulate glucose production and fullness.
- **Acetate** – enters the blood and is used by various tissues.

These fatty acids do something remarkable: they bind to receptors on special gut cells (**L cells**) and stimulate them to release **GLP-1** and **PYY**, two fullness hormones. In other words, **fibre tells you, through your bacteria, that you are full**.

## Fibre "for the bacteria"

Researchers talk about **microbiota-accessible carbohydrates**. The best ones:

- **inulin** and **fructo-oligosaccharides**: onion, garlic, leek, chicory, Jerusalem artichoke, greener bananas,
- **resistant starch**: potatoes and rice cooked and cooled, pulses, green bananas, oats,
- **beta-glucans**: oats, barley,
- **pectin**: apples, pears, quinces, citrus fruit,
- **fibre from pulses**: beans, lentils, chickpeas, peas.

## The 30 plants rule

A large "citizen science" study (American Gut, McDonald, 2018) of more than 10,000 people observed that those who ate **more than 30 different types of plants a week** had a **more diverse** microbiome than those who ate 10 or fewer. It is an observational study, not proof that 30 is a magic number, but it is a good practical target.

**What counts:** vegetables, fruit, pulses, whole grains, nuts, seeds, herbs and spices. Each different type = 1 point (for example, red and green peppers may count separately, depending on how you count; what matters is variety).

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** it is easier than it sounds? A Romanian country-style vegetable soup can have 8–10 plants on its own: onion, carrot, parsnip, celeriac, pepper, tomatoes, potatoes, green beans, lovage, parsley.

> [!WARNING]
> **Caution:** if you have **irritable bowel syndrome** or other digestive problems, some fermentable fibres (onion, garlic, pulses) can cause bloating and pain. Talk to a gastroenterologist or dietitian about a personalised approach.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** for one week, count the different plants you eat. Start easy: a seed mix on your yoghurt, a handful of herbs in your salad and one new pulse each week.

## In short

- Bacteria ferment fibre and produce butyrate, propionate and acetate.
- These fatty acids feed the gut and stimulate GLP-1 and PYY (fullness).
- Practical target: 30 different plants a week.
