# Romanian fermented foods: sauerkraut, kefir, borș

Our grandparents did not know what the microbiome was, but every autumn they put cabbage up to ferment. Science is only now starting to prove them right.

## The Stanford study

In 2021, researchers at Stanford University (Wastyk et al., *Cell*) split 36 healthy adults into two groups for 10 weeks:

- one group ate **lots of fermented foods** (yoghurt, kefir, fermented cheese, sauerkraut, kimchi, kombucha, vegetable brine), reaching ~6 servings a day,
- the other ate **lots of fibre**.

The results:

- The fermented-food group had an **increase in microbiome diversity** and a **decrease in 19 inflammation markers** in the blood (including interleukin-6).
- The fibre group **did not** see more diversity in this short period, although fibre has other proven benefits.

It is a small study, but very well controlled. It suggests fermented foods and fibre **complement each other**.

## Fermented foods in Romanian cooking

| Food | What it brings | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| **Sauerkraut** (in brine) | lactic acid bacteria, vitamin C, fibre | lots of salt |
| **Brine pickles** (cucumbers, green tomatoes, cauliflower, peppers) | lactic acid bacteria | lots of salt |
| **Borș** (fermented bran) | bacteria and yeasts | usually boiled in soup, and heat kills most live bacteria |
| **Kefir** | very diverse bacteria and yeasts, protein, calcium | – |
| **Yoghurt** (with live cultures) | lactic acid bacteria, protein, calcium | choose plain versions without sugar |
| **Sana, buttermilk** | lactic acid bacteria | – |
| **Aged cheeses** (cașcaval, brânză de burduf) | some bacteria, calcium, protein | salt and saturated fat |
| **Sourdough bread** | fermentation reduces phytates and may lower the glucose response | the bacteria do not survive baking |

> [!WARNING]
> **The important difference:** pickles made **in vinegar** and **pasteurised** pickles from the shop **no longer contain live bacteria**. For the effect on the microbiome, choose pickles made **in brine**, unpasteurised, or make them at home.

> [!CAUTION]
> Romanian fermented foods are often **very salty**. If you have high blood pressure or kidney disease, eat small portions, rinse sauerkraut, or choose dairy options (kefir, yoghurt). WHO recommends less than 5 g of salt a day.

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** sauerkraut juice was traditionally used in Romania as a "remedy" after heavy meals? Science does not confirm any miraculous properties, but it does contain live lactic acid bacteria and a little vitamin C.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** try 1–2 servings of fermented foods a day: a glass of kefir at breakfast, a few spoonfuls of sauerkraut at lunch. Increase gradually to avoid bloating.

## In short

- Fermented foods increased microbiome diversity and lowered inflammation in a Stanford study.
- Sauerkraut, brine pickles, kefir and yoghurt are cheap, Romanian options.
- Watch the salt; vinegar or pasteurised pickles have no live bacteria.
