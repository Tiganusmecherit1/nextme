# What harms your microbiome: antibiotics, ultra-processed food, sweeteners

## 1. Antibiotics

Antibiotics save lives, but they do not tell bad bacteria from good ones. A course can sharply reduce microbiome diversity.

In one study (Dethlefsen and Relman, 2011), after a course of ciprofloxacin, most participants' microbiomes **largely recovered within a few weeks**, but **not completely**, and some species remained reduced for months. Recovery differed from person to person.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Do not refuse or stop a course of antibiotics** prescribed by your doctor out of concern for your microbiome. Untreated infections are far more dangerous. What you can do: take antibiotics **only when your doctor considers them necessary** (not for viral colds), finish the course as instructed, and eat fibre and fermented foods afterwards.

## 2. A diet low in fibre and high in ultra-processed food

When they get no fibre, gut bacteria "go hungry". Some start eating the **mucus layer** that protects the gut wall. Animal studies (Sonnenburg, 2016) showed that a low-fibre diet over several generations **wipes out bacterial species** that do not come back even when fibre is reintroduced.

Ultra-processed food (processed meats, industrial pastries, snacks, sweets, sugary drinks, ready meals) usually has **little fibre** and many additives. Some emulsifiers have shown negative effects on gut mucus in animal studies and in small human studies; research is ongoing.

## 3. Artificial sweeteners

A 2022 study (Suez, *Cell*) gave healthy adults saccharin, sucralose, aspartame or stevia, in doses below the permitted limit, for 2 weeks. The results:

- all sweeteners **changed the microbiome**,
- **saccharin and sucralose** affected **glucose tolerance** in some participants,
- the effects were **very different from person to person**.

In 2023, WHO published a **conditional** recommendation **not to use non-sugar sweeteners for weight control**, because over the long term they do not seem to help with weight loss.

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** this does not mean a "zero" drink is as bad as a sugary one? For someone who drinks litres of sugary soda every day, switching to the sugar-free version can be a useful intermediate step. The final goal is still **water**.

## 4. Too much alcohol

Heavy drinking can increase gut permeability and unbalance the microbiome.

## 5. Chronic stress and short sleep

The gut and brain communicate in both directions (next lesson). Chronic stress and disrupted sleep are associated with changes in the microbiome, although cause and effect are hard to separate.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** you do not have to be perfect. The basic rule: **more plants, more fibre, something fermented, less ultra-processed food**. That makes up for a lot of the rest.

## In short

- Antibiotics disrupt the microbiome, but are necessary when your doctor recommends them.
- Lack of fibre and ultra-processed food "starve" the good bacteria.
- Sweeteners can change the microbiome differently in different people; WHO does not recommend them for weight loss.
