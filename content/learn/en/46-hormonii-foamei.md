# Ghrelin and leptin: the hormones of hunger and reserves

Hunger is not just "an empty stomach". It is a conversation between the stomach, gut, fat tissue and brain, carried out through hormones. Two of the most important are **ghrelin** and **leptin**.

## Ghrelin: "eat now"

- It is made mainly by the **stomach**.
- It **rises before meals** and falls after you eat.
- It adapts to your schedule: if you eat at 13:00 every day, ghrelin starts rising before 13:00. That is why you get hungry "at mealtime".
- It rises when you **sleep little** and when you are on a strict diet.

Good news: a wave of ghrelin-driven hunger usually **passes** within 20–30 minutes if you do not eat. It does not rise forever.

## Leptin: "we have reserves"

- It is made by **fat tissue**, in proportion to the amount of fat.
- It tells the brain (hypothalamus) **how full the reserves are** in the long term.
- When you lose weight, leptin **falls**, and the brain reads this as a threat: hunger rises and energy expenditure drops slightly.

## The leptin paradox

If fat makes leptin and leptin reduces hunger, why aren't people with obesity always full? Because in many people with obesity the brain becomes **less sensitive to leptin** (leptin resistance). Leptin is high, but the message no longer gets through well.

## Sleep and hunger

In one study (Spiegel, 2004), healthy young men who slept only **4 hours a night for two nights in a row** had:

- **leptin ~18% lower**,
- **ghrelin ~28% higher**,
- **hunger ~24% higher**, especially for sweet, salty and starchy foods.

This explains why, after a bad night, you crave pastries and sweets.

## Other hormones that make you full

- **GLP-1** and **PYY** – from the gut, released after a meal (next lesson).
- **Cholecystokinin (CCK)** – from the small intestine, released especially after fat and protein.
- **Insulin** – also has a small fullness effect in the brain.

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** fullness signals need **15–20 minutes** to reach the brain? If you eat very quickly, you can finish the plate before the "I'm full" message arrives.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** eat more slowly (put your fork down between bites), sleep 7+ hours, include protein and fibre at every meal and keep a fairly regular meal schedule. All of these work with your hormones, not against them.

## In short

- Ghrelin (from the stomach) raises hunger before meals; the wave passes in 20–30 minutes.
- Leptin (from fat) tells the brain how full the reserves are.
- Short sleep lowers leptin, raises ghrelin and makes you crave sweets.
