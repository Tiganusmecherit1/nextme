# How to boost GLP-1 naturally (and why the effect is modest)

Online you will see the idea of a "natural Ozempic": a tea, a supplement or a food that supposedly does the same as the drugs. **It does not exist.** But through diet and habits you can support your natural GLP-1 production, and that contributes to fullness and steadier blood sugar.

## What stimulates natural GLP-1

**1. Protein.** The amino acids in protein are among the strongest stimuli for L cells. In one study (Jakubowicz, 2014), people with type 2 diabetes who took **50 g of whey protein before breakfast** had higher GLP-1 and lower blood sugar after the meal.

**2. Good fats.** Fatty acids (especially unsaturated ones) also stimulate GLP-1, and fats slow stomach emptying.

**3. Fermentable fibre.** Bacteria in the colon turn it into short-chain fatty acids, which activate receptors on L cells and increase GLP-1 and PYY secretion. The effect builds over **weeks**, as the microbiome adapts.

**4. Food order.** In a Japanese study (Kuwata, 2016), when participants ate **fish or meat before rice**, GLP-1 levels were higher, stomach emptying slower and blood sugar lower than when they ate the rice first.

**5. Whole meals, chewed slowly.** Solid, minimally processed foods that reach the gut gradually stimulate GLP-1 better than liquid or ultra-processed ones.

**6. Movement and weight.** Regular physical activity and weight loss may improve gut hormone responses, although the data are less clear.

## How big the effect is

Let us be honest:

- Natural GLP-1 rises **several times** above its resting level after a meal and falls back within a few hours.
- The drugs keep GLP-1 receptors activated **much more strongly, continuously, for days**.
- So no diet **will reproduce** the 15% weight losses seen in the semaglutide studies.

That does not mean it is not worth it. It means natural GLP-1 is **one piece** of a bigger puzzle: protein, fibre, plants, sleep, movement.

> [!WARNING]
> **Myth vs reality:** "berberine is nature's Ozempic". Berberine has a few studies for blood sugar, but its effect on weight is small and **not comparable** to GLP-1 drugs. It can interact with medications. Details in the **Supplements** module.

## The "GLP-1-friendly" plate

1. **Start** with vegetables or a vegetable soup.
2. **Continue** with protein: eggs, fish, chicken, cheese, beans, lentils.
3. **Add** a good fat: olive oil, nuts, avocado.
4. **Finish** with carbohydrates: wholemeal bread, potatoes, rice, polenta.
5. Eat **slowly**, for at least 15–20 minutes.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** a breakfast of 2–3 eggs, vegetables and a slice of wholemeal bread usually keeps you full much longer than a croissant with coffee, at the same calories. Part of the difference is GLP-1.

## In short

- Protein, good fats, fibre and food order stimulate natural GLP-1.
- The effect is real, but **much smaller** than that of the drugs.
- There is no "natural Ozempic"; there is a plate that fills you up better.
