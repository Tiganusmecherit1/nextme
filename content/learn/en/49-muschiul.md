# How a muscle is built

Skeletal muscles (the ones you move voluntarily) make up about **38% of body weight in men** and **31% in women**. You have more than 600 of them. They are the largest metabolic organ in the body.

## From big to small

Think of a muscle as a **thick electrical cable**:

1. **The whole muscle** (for example, the biceps) is wrapped in a membrane and continues into the **tendons**, which attach to the bones.
2. Inside are **fascicles**, like bundles of wires.
3. Each fascicle contains **muscle fibres**. A fibre is a single cell, very long (it can be several centimetres or more) and with **many nuclei**.
4. Each fibre is packed with **myofibrils**, the threads that contract.
5. Myofibrils are divided into **sarcomeres**, the "units" of contraction.
6. Inside the sarcomeres are two proteins: **actin** (thin filaments) and **myosin** (thick filaments, with "heads" that grab on).

## How it contracts

1. The brain sends an electrical signal down the nerve.
2. The signal reaches the muscle fibre and releases **calcium** inside it.
3. Calcium "unlocks" the actin.
4. The **myosin** heads grab the actin and pull it, like rowers in a boat.
5. Each pull uses one molecule of **ATP**.
6. The sarcomeres shorten, the fibre shortens, the muscle contracts and pulls on the bone.

## Fibre types

- **Type I fibres (slow, "red")** – lots of mitochondria, fatigue-resistant, used for walking, posture, long runs.
- **Type II fibres (fast, "white")** – stronger and faster, but tire more quickly. Used for sprints, jumps, heavy lifts. They grow the most in size with strength training and are lost fastest with age.

The ratio between them is partly genetic, but training can change their characteristics.

## The motor unit

A motor nerve and all the fibres it controls form a **motor unit**. When you lift something light, the brain activates only a few units. When you lift something heavy or get close to maximal effort, it activates almost all of them. That is why **effort close to your limit** matters for muscle growth.

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** muscle is also a gland? When it contracts, it releases hundreds of substances called **myokines**, which affect the liver, fat, bones, brain and immune system. Working muscle talks to the whole body.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** the first 4–8 weeks of training bring a lot of strength "from the brain": the nervous system learns to activate more motor units and coordinate them. Muscle grows visibly later. Do not give up in the first month.

## In short

- Muscle = fascicles → fibres → myofibrils → sarcomeres with actin and myosin.
- Calcium and ATP make myosin pull on actin.
- Fast fibres grow the most with strength training and are the first to go with age.
