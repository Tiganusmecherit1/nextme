# What happens in your body when you lift a weight

You pick up a dumbbell and do 10 curls. In those 30 seconds, dozens of things happen in your body. Here are the most important, step by step.

## During the set (seconds)

- **The brain** recruits more and more motor units as fatigue builds.
- **Myosin pulls on actin**, using ATP. The fast system (phosphocreatine) runs out in ~10 seconds, then glycolysis takes over.
- **Glucose "doors" (GLUT4)** open in the muscle **without insulin**. The muscle pulls sugar from the blood.
- Blood vessels widen and blood rushes into the muscle: the **"pump"** feeling.
- By-products of effort build up, and towards the end of the set you feel the **burn**.

## The main signal: mechanical tension

The most important thing a muscle "feels" is **mechanical tension**: the force that stretches and loads it while it works. Sensors in the fibre's membrane and inside it turn this force into chemical signals (a process called **mechanotransduction**).

These signals activate a "control centre" in the cell called **mTOR**, which tells the ribosomes: **"build muscle protein"**.

## After training (hours)

- **Muscle protein synthesis rises** and stays raised for about **24–48 hours** (Phillips, 1997). If you eat protein during this window, building outpaces breakdown.
- **Satellite cells** (the muscle's "stem" cells) are activated and can donate new nuclei to fibres so they can grow.
- The muscle releases **myokines** into the blood, for example **interleukin-6**, which reaches the liver and fat and helps mobilise energy.
- Glycogen is restored, and the muscle stays **more sensitive to insulin** for up to 1–2 days.

## Hormones: less important than you thought

After a hard session, testosterone and growth hormone rise temporarily. For a long time these rises were thought to be the key. One study (West, 2010) showed that **these short spikes did not affect** muscle or strength gains. What matters is the **local signal in the muscle** (tension) and the **protein** in your food.

## The following days

- **Micro-damage** in the fibres is repaired. **Muscle soreness** may appear (see the lesson on DOMS).
- **Bone** also gets the signal to strengthen (the **Bones** chapter).
- **Tendons** start producing more collagen.
- Repeated week after week, the muscle becomes **bigger, stronger** and has **more mitochondria**.

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** muscle does not grow during training, but **during recovery**? Training is just the signal. Food, sleep and rest do the building.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** for a strong signal, get close to maximal effort: end the set when you have **1–3 reps "in reserve"**. Then eat a meal with 20–40 g of protein in the following hours and sleep well.

## In short

- During the set: ATP, glucose taken up without insulin, motor unit recruitment.
- The key signal is mechanical tension, which switches on mTOR and protein synthesis.
- Building happens over the next 24–48 hours, with protein and sleep.
