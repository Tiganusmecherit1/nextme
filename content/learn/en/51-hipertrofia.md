# How muscle grows: progressive overload

The body is very economical. Muscle uses energy, so the body does not build it "for the fun of it". It builds it only when it gets the **repeated signal** that it is needed.

## The basic principle

**Progressive overload** means gradually asking the muscle for **a little more** than it is used to. You can do this through:

- **more weight** (the best-known method),
- **more reps** with the same weight,
- **more sets** per week,
- **better technique** and a fuller range of motion,
- **shorter rests** (less important for growth).

If you do the same exercises for months, with the same weight and the same number of reps, the muscle has no reason to adapt.

## What the guidelines and studies say

**Reps and weight:** a wide range works for muscle growth. An analysis (Schoenfeld, 2017) showed that both heavy weights (~6–12 reps) and lighter ones (~20–30 reps) produce **similar muscle growth, as long as sets are taken close to maximal effort**. Heavy weights do, however, bring **more strength**.

**Weekly volume:** how many sets you do per week for a muscle group matters. Another analysis (Schoenfeld, 2017) suggested that **10 or more sets per week** for a muscle give bigger gains than fewer than 5 sets. For beginners, even less works.

**Frequency:** ACSM recommends training each muscle group **2–3 times a week** for beginners. For advanced lifters, total volume matters more.

**WHO minimum for health:** strength exercises for all major muscle groups on **at least 2 days a week**.

## A simple plan to start

| Element | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Days per week | 2–3, full body |
| Exercises | 5–8 basic movements (squats, presses, rows/pulls, deadlifts, lunges, hip thrusts) |
| Sets | 2–3 per exercise |
| Reps | 8–15 |
| Effort | the last rep is hard, with 1–3 reps "in reserve" |
| Rest between sets | 1.5–3 minutes |
| Progress | when you hit the top of the rep range on every set, increase the weight by 2.5–5% |

## How fast it grows

The pace is slow. A well-trained, well-fed beginner can gain **about 0.5–1 kg of muscle per month** in the first year, women usually somewhat less in absolute terms. The pace slows with experience. Any promise of "5 kg of muscle in a month" is not realistic.

> [!WARNING]
> **Myth vs reality:** "women who lift weights get bulky". Women have on average 10–20 times less testosterone than men. Strength training makes them stronger, more toned and gives them sturdier bones, not "bulky", without years of dedicated training and, often, without substances.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** log your weights and reps in NextMe. What you do not measure, you cannot progressively increase.

## In short

- Muscle grows only if it gradually gets a bigger signal.
- Both heavy and light weights work, if sets are close to maximal effort.
- 2–3 sessions a week, ~10 sets per muscle, progress logged.
