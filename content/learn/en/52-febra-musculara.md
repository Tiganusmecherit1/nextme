# Muscle soreness: what it is and what it isn't

Muscle soreness (DOMS, "delayed onset muscle soreness") is the pain and stiffness that appear **12–24 hours** after a new or intense workout, peak at **24–72 hours** and usually go away within a few days.

## What causes it

- **Micro-damage** in the muscle fibres and the connective tissue around them.
- The **inflammation** that follows, as part of the repair process.
- **Sensitisation** of the nerve endings in the muscle and its sheath.

It appears especially after:

- **new** exercises or ones you are not used to,
- the **lowering** phase of a movement (eccentric): lowering into a squat, lowering a dumbbell, walking downhill.

## What it is NOT

**It is not lactic acid.** The lactate produced during exercise clears from the muscle within about **an hour** after training. It cannot be what hurts two days later.

**It is not a reliable sign of growth.** Soreness drops a lot after the first few sessions of the same exercise (the "repeated bout effect"), but the muscle keeps growing. You can make great progress without soreness, and have lots of soreness without progress.

## What helps (and what doesn't really)

Studies show that:

- **Light movement** (walking, easy cycling, light-weight exercises) temporarily reduces the pain.
- **Massage** seems to reduce pain a little.
- **Sleep and protein** support recovery.
- **Stretching** before or after training **does not prevent** soreness, according to studies.
- **Anti-inflammatories** can reduce pain but, used often, may interfere with adaptation; do not take them "preventively".

> [!CAUTION]
> **When it is NOT normal soreness:** very severe pain, a lot of muscle swelling, severe weakness and **dark, cola-coloured urine** after an extreme workout. These can be signs of **rhabdomyolysis**, a medical emergency. See a doctor immediately. Also, a sudden, sharp pain during an exercise can be a strain or tear, not soreness.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** when you start a new programme, do **fewer sets** in the first week (for example, half). You will avoid heavy soreness and be able to train again in 2–3 days.

## In short

- Soreness appears at 12–24 h and lasts up to 72 h; it comes from micro-damage and inflammation.
- It is not lactic acid and does not necessarily show the muscle is growing.
- Light movement and sleep help; dark urine after an extreme effort is an emergency.
