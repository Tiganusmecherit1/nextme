# Muscle after 40, 50, 60+: sarcopenia

**Sarcopenia** means the loss of muscle mass, strength and function with age. The European Working Group on Sarcopenia (EWGSOP2) considers it a **muscle disease**, with a big impact on quality of life.

## How fast it is lost

- After about **30–40 years**, inactive people lose on average **3–8% of their muscle mass per decade**.
- After **60**, the pace speeds up.
- **Strength** falls even faster than muscle mass, especially in the fast fibres.

## Why it matters

Muscle is not just about looks. It is:

- **insurance against falls** and fractures,
- the ability to climb stairs, get up from a chair, carry shopping bags, play with your grandchildren,
- the **sponge for blood sugar** (less muscle, higher diabetes risk),
- a protein reserve in case of illness or hospital stays.

Grip strength and walking speed are among the best predictors of health and longevity in older adults.

## Test yourself at home

A test doctors use (the chair-stand test): sit on a chair without arms, arms crossed over your chest, and stand up and sit down **5 times**, as fast as you safely can. EWGSOP2 uses a time of **more than 15 seconds** as a sign of low leg strength.

## The best news: it is never too late

In a famous 1990 study (Fiatarone, *JAMA*), people aged **86–96** in a nursing home did leg strength training for **8 weeks**. The result: strength rose on average by **174%**, and walking speed improved. Some participants gave up their walking sticks.

Muscles respond to training **at any age**. They just need:

- **strength training** at least twice a week,
- **more protein** per meal (25–40 g; see the lesson on protein needs),
- **balance and coordination**: WHO recommends balance and strength activities for older adults on at least **3 days a week**,
- enough **vitamin D**.

## By age

- **40–50 years:** now is the time to build your "reserve". 2–3 strength sessions a week.
- **50–60 years:** maintain strength; add balance and power exercises (faster, controlled movements). In women, menopause speeds up the loss of muscle and bone.
- **60+ years:** strength, balance, daily walking. Start with body weight, resistance bands and light dumbbells, and progress gradually.

> [!CAUTION]
> If you are over 60, or have heart disease, diagnosed osteoporosis, a joint replacement or joint pain, talk to your doctor or a physiotherapist before you start. An adapted programme is safe and extremely useful.

> **In practice:** enter your real age and any injuries or conditions in NextMe, and if you are a beginner, start with the "Health and energy" or "Home workouts" goals.

## In short

- After 30–40 you lose 3–8% of your muscle per decade if you do nothing.
- Low strength raises the risk of falls, fractures and diabetes.
- Strength training works even at 90, with enough protein.
