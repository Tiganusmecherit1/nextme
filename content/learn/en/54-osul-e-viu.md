# Bone is alive: it is constantly torn down and rebuilt

When you think of bones, you probably picture a dry white skeleton. In your body, bone is **living tissue**, with blood vessels, nerves and cells that work non-stop.

## What it is made of

- **Collagen** (a protein) – the flexible "reinforcement", like the steel bars in concrete.
- **Minerals** (mainly calcium and phosphate, as hydroxyapatite) – the "concrete" that gives hardness.
- **Water** and **cells**.

The combination of collagen and mineral makes bone both **hard** and **slightly flexible**. That is why healthy bone can bear very heavy loads without breaking.

## The three cell types

1. **Osteoclasts – the demolishers.** They dissolve small pieces of old or damaged bone.
2. **Osteoblasts – the builders.** They follow the demolishers and lay down new bone: first collagen, then mineral.
3. **Osteocytes – the sensors.** They are former osteoblasts that got "trapped" in the bone. They are **the most numerous bone cells** and form a huge network of microscopic channels throughout the skeleton. They **sense the forces** pressing on the bone and decide where it needs strengthening and where it needs demolishing.

## Bone remodelling

Demolishers and builders work as a team, in small "building sites" spread throughout the skeleton. The process is called **remodelling** and it:

- repairs the **micro-cracks** that form every day from movement,
- adapts the shape and thickness of bone to the forces it faces,
- releases or stores **calcium** for the rest of the body.

Every year, about **10% of the skeleton** is rebuilt. In theory, you have an almost "new" skeleton every ~10 years.

## The balance across life

- **Childhood and youth:** the builders win. Bone mass rises to a peak, usually reached between **20 and 30**.
- **30–50 years:** roughly in balance.
- **After 50**, and especially in women after menopause (when oestrogen, which brakes the demolishers, falls): the demolishers win. Bone becomes thinner and more porous.

When the loss is large, **osteoporosis** develops. The International Osteoporosis Foundation estimates that after 50, about **1 in 3 women** and **1 in 5 men** will have a fracture due to osteoporosis.

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** astronauts in space can lose about **1–2% of bone density** at the hip and spine every month? Without gravity, osteocytes no longer feel the load and send the signal "we don't need this much bone". This shows how much bone depends on **loading**.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** bone needs three things: **loading** (movement, weights, impact), **materials** (protein, calcium, vitamin D) and **hormones** in balance. The next lesson explains exactly what happens when you lift.

## In short

- Bone is alive: collagen + mineral + cells.
- Osteoclasts demolish, osteoblasts build, osteocytes sense forces.
- ~10% of the skeleton is rebuilt each year; after 50, losses can win out.
