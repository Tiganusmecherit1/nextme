# Tendons and joints: they adapt more slowly

## Tendons: the ropes between muscle and bone

Tendons connect muscles to bones. They are made mostly of **collagen**, arranged in parallel fibres, like a rope. They are very resistant to pulling, but have **few blood vessels**, so their metabolism is slow.

**When you train:**

- Loading increases **collagen synthesis** in the tendon in the days after training.
- Over the long term (months), the tendon becomes **stiffer** (in the good sense: it transmits force more efficiently) and, in some cases, thicker.

**The problem:** muscle gets stronger in a few weeks, but tendons need **months**. If weights go up too fast, the muscle can pull harder than the tendon is ready for. That is how **tendinopathies** happen (pain in the Achilles tendon, in the knee below the kneecap, at the elbow).

## Cartilage and joints

The ends of bones are covered with **cartilage**, a smooth, elastic tissue that allows gliding. Cartilage **has no blood vessels**: it is nourished by the fluid in the joint, which is "pumped" into it through movement and loading, like a sponge being squeezed and released.

That is why **movement is good for joints**. An analysis of studies (Bricca, 2019) found no evidence that therapeutic exercise harms knee cartilage, even in people at risk of or with osteoarthritis. On the contrary, exercise reduces pain and improves function.

> [!WARNING]
> **Myth vs reality:** "squats ruin your knees". Done correctly, with appropriate weight and gradual progress, squats **strengthen** the muscles and tissues around the knee. Problems usually come from sudden jumps in volume or weight, poor technique or an existing injury.

## Good pain vs bad pain in tendons and joints

- **Acceptable:** mild discomfort (up to 3 on a 0–10 scale) during the exercise, which is gone by the next day.
- **Stop signal:** sharp pain, pain that increases from set to set, pain that persists or is worse **the next morning**, swelling, locking, instability.

## Protection rules

1. **Increase weight or volume gradually** (for example, by no more than ~10% a week in total volume).
2. **Warm up** for 5–10 minutes beforehand, with light movement and light sets of the main exercise.
3. **Vary** exercises and angles.
4. **Leave 48 hours** between hard sessions for the same muscle group.
5. **Sleep and eat enough protein**; tendon collagen is also built from amino acids.

> [!CAUTION]
> Persistent joint pain, swelling, warmth or redness of a joint, locking, or a feeling that the knee "gives way" should be assessed by an orthopaedic doctor or physiotherapist.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** if you have a sensitive area (knee, shoulder, back), add it under injuries in your NextMe profile and choose exercise variations that load it more gently.

## In short

- Tendons adapt in months, not weeks; progress gradually.
- Cartilage is nourished by movement; correct exercise is good for joints.
- Mild discomfort is acceptable; sharp pain or pain that is worse the next day is a stop signal.
