# Sleep: the most underrated supplement

If there were a pill that increased your strength, reduced your hunger, steadied your blood sugar and improved your memory and mood, everyone would take it. It exists. It is called **sleep**.

## How much you should sleep

The American Academy of Sleep Medicine recommends **7 hours or more per night** for adults, on a regular basis. Some people need 8–9. Very few (under 1%) truly function well on less than 6.

## What happens during sleep

Sleep runs in cycles of about 90 minutes, with different stages:

- **Deep (slow-wave) sleep** – mostly in the first half of the night. The most **growth hormone** is released, tissues are repaired and memory for movements is consolidated.
- **REM sleep (with dreams)** – mostly towards morning. Important for memory, emotions and learning.
- During sleep, the spaces between brain cells widen, and cerebrospinal fluid "washes away" the waste built up during the day more efficiently (animal studies, Xie, 2013).

## Sleep and weight loss

In one study (Nedeltcheva, 2010), overweight adults followed the same calorie-deficit diet twice, for 2 weeks each: once sleeping **8.5 hours**, once **5.5 hours**. They lost about the same weight, but:

- with 8.5 hours, **more of the weight lost was fat**,
- with 5.5 hours, they lost **~55% less fat** and **more fat-free tissue** (including muscle),
- and they were **hungrier**.

## Sleep and blood sugar

After just a few nights of short sleep, insulin sensitivity drops (see the lesson on blood sugar spikes). And short sleep raises ghrelin and lowers leptin, which makes you crave sweets and pastries.

## Sleep and training

Short sleep reduces:

- strength and power,
- reaction time and coordination,
- motivation,
- muscle recovery.

And it raises the risk of injury.

> [!NOTE]
> **Did you know** bright, blue light in the evening delays the release of **melatonin**, the hormone that tells your body it is night? Bright light **in the morning**, on the other hand, "sets" your internal clock and helps you fall asleep more easily in the evening.

## Sleep hygiene

1. **A consistent schedule**, including weekends (no more than an hour's difference).
2. **Natural light in the morning**, 10–30 minutes outside.
3. **Caffeine** at least **8 hours** before bed (its half-life is ~5 hours).
4. **Alcohol** makes you fall asleep but disrupts sleep in the second half of the night.
5. **A cool (~18–19°C), dark, quiet bedroom.**
6. **Screens** put away 30–60 minutes before bed.
7. **Large meals** 2–3 hours before bed.
8. **Intense workouts** preferably not right before sleep (though many people sleep fine after evening training).

> [!CAUTION]
> Loud snoring, pauses in breathing during sleep (noticed by a partner), strong daytime sleepiness or insomnia lasting more than 3 months should be discussed with a doctor. **Sleep apnoea** is common, especially in people who are overweight, and it is treatable.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** if you want just one change this week, **go to bed 30 minutes earlier**. It is the cheapest performance upgrade you can make.

## In short

- Adults need 7+ hours of regular sleep.
- Short sleep raises hunger, lowers insulin sensitivity and makes weight loss burn muscle instead of fat.
- Consistent schedule, morning light, no caffeine after lunch, a cool and dark bedroom.
