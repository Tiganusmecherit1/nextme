# Body signals: hunger, cravings, fatigue, pain

Your body has an extremely fine messaging system. The better you understand it, the less you depend on rules and apps. Here are the most important signals.

## Real hunger vs cravings

| Physical hunger | Craving (emotional hunger) |
|---|---|
| comes on **gradually** | comes on **suddenly** |
| you would eat **any** filling food | you want something **specific** (sweet, salty, crunchy) |
| you feel it in your **stomach** (empty, rumbling) | you feel it more "in your head" |
| goes away after you eat | can continue even after you are full |
| appears a few hours after a meal | appears with boredom, stress, sadness, tiredness |

**The quick test:** "Would I eat a boiled egg or a plate of beans right now?" If yes, it is hunger. If you only want chocolate, it is more likely a craving.

A craving is not a weakness. It is a signal that you need something: rest, comfort, a break, company. Sometimes a glass of water, a 5-minute walk or a message to a friend is enough.

## Thirst

Thirst is sometimes mistaken for hunger. Before an unplanned snack, drink a glass of water and wait 10 minutes.

## Normal tiredness vs overtraining

**Normal:**

- you feel tired after a hard session and **recover within 1–2 days**,
- you get mild soreness after new exercises.

**Signs you need more rest (overreaching):**

- performance **drops for several weeks**, even though you train consistently,
- your morning **resting heart rate** is consistently higher than usual,
- you **sleep badly** even though you are tired,
- irritability, lack of motivation, frequent colds,
- aches that do not go away.

The consensus of European and American experts (Meeusen, 2013) shows that true **overtraining syndrome** is rare in amateurs, but **overreaching** (too much training, too little rest, too little food) is common. The fix: a lighter week, more sleep, more food.

> [!WARNING]
> **Too little food + lots of exercise.** The International Olympic Committee describes **Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (REDs)**: when you eat far too little for how much you train, you can get missed periods, fragile bones, stress fractures, lower immunity and lower performance. This can happen to amateurs too, women and men.

## Good pain vs bad pain

**"Good" pain (usually fine):**

- the **burn** in the muscle towards the end of a set, which goes away within seconds,
- diffuse **muscle soreness** 1–3 days later,
- mild discomfort that warms up and eases during the session.

**"Bad" pain (stop):**

- **sharp, stabbing**, in one specific spot,
- in a **joint**, not a muscle,
- that **increases** with each rep,
- that **wakes you up at night**,
- with **swelling, bruising, numbness** or tingling,
- that makes you **change the way you walk** or move.

## Emergency signals

> [!CAUTION]
> **Stop exercising immediately and call for medical help (112 in the EU)** if you have: **pain or pressure in the chest**, especially spreading to the arm, jaw or back; unusual **shortness of breath**; **severe dizziness or fainting**; very fast or irregular **palpitations**; **sudden weakness** on one side of the body, slurred speech or a drooping mouth. After an extreme effort, **very dark, cola-coloured urine** with severe muscle pain is also an emergency.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** each morning, note how you slept and how you feel (1 to 5). In a few weeks you will see the patterns: which days go well, what tires you, when you get cravings.

## In short

- Hunger comes on gradually and accepts any food; a craving comes suddenly and wants something specific.
- Normal tiredness passes in 1–2 days; performance that drops for weeks calls for rest.
- Sharp, joint or increasing pain is a stop signal; chest pain is an emergency.
