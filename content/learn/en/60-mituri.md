# 12 myths busted

## 1. "You need a detox to get rid of toxins"

**Reality:** you already have a very effective detox system: **your liver, kidneys, lungs, gut and skin**. A critical review of the studies (Klein and Kiat, 2015) found no solid evidence that "detox" diets or products remove toxins. The rapid weight loss from juice cleanses is mostly water and glycogen.

## 2. "Fasted cardio burns more fat"

**Reality:** during the session, you burn a bit more fat if you have not eaten. But over the day, the body compensates. One study (Schoenfeld, 2014) compared fasted cardio with cardio after a meal, at the same calories: **fat loss was similar**. Choose whichever feels better for you.

## 3. "Carbs at night turn into fat"

**Reality:** your body does not have a clock that turns rice into fat after 6 pm. What matters is the **daily calorie total**. Some people even sleep better with a carbohydrate-containing dinner. Still, very large meals right before bed can affect sleep and digestion.

## 4. "Metabolism slows down at 30"

**Reality:** Pontzer's study (2021) of more than 6,000 people showed that metabolism adjusted for body size is **almost constant between 20 and 60**. What changes is movement, muscle mass and habits.

## 5. "You have to eat every 3 hours to keep your metabolism going"

**Reality:** a meta-analysis (Schoenfeld, 2015) found no clear advantage of frequent meals for weight loss, at the same calories. 3 meals or 5 meals: choose what suits you. For muscle, what matters is splitting **protein** into 3–4 servings.

## 6. "Crunches burn belly fat"

**Reality:** you cannot spot-reduce (see the lesson "Why you can't lose fat from the spot you choose"). Crunches strengthen muscles, but fat leaves from the whole body, in an order decided by genetics and hormones.

## 7. "Fat turns into muscle"

**Reality:** they are different tissues. Burned fat becomes mainly **CO₂ that you breathe out** and water (Meerman, 2014). Muscle is built separately, from amino acids.

## 8. "Sweat means fat burned"

**Reality:** sweat is water and salts that cool you down. Saunas, cling film on your belly and heavy clothes make you lose **water**, which comes back with the first glass.

## 9. "Lots of protein damages the kidneys"

**Reality:** in **healthy** people, analyses of the studies (Devries, 2018) found no harm to kidney function at higher protein intakes. If you already have kidney disease, talk to your doctor.

## 10. "Women who lift weights get bulky"

**Reality:** women have much less testosterone than men. Strength training makes them stronger, with sturdier bones and a better metabolism.

## 11. "Gluten-free is healthier for everyone"

**Reality:** for people with **coeliac disease** (about 1% of the population) or diagnosed sensitivity, cutting out gluten is necessary. For everyone else, there is no evidence it is healthier. Many "gluten-free" products are ultra-processed, with more sugar and fat and less fibre.

## 12. "No pain, no gain"

**Reality:** muscle soreness is not a reliable sign of progress. Muscle grows from **repeated, progressive mechanical tension, with protein and sleep**, not from pain.

> [!TIP]
> **In practice:** when you hear a new "rule" at the gym or online, ask: **"What's the study?"** If the answer is "everyone says so", it is probably a myth.

> The information in NextMe is educational and does not replace advice from a doctor, dietitian or physiotherapist.

## In short

- Detoxes, fasted cardio and "carbs at night" do not have the promised effects.
- Metabolism is stable between 20 and 60; spot reduction does not exist.
- Always ask: "What's the study?"
